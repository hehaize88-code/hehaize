An effective return enquiry begins with the order stage and a specific problem. An item that has not been purchased, one waiting in a warehouse and one already delivered overseas present different practical situations. Treating them as the same “refund request” can leave essential information out of the conversation.

This guide explains how to prepare a clear case and follow it through. It does not promise that every seller accepts returns, impose a universal deadline or guarantee that every cost will be refunded. Those details must be confirmed for the particular order and service.

## Check eligibility before buying

If the ability to return an item matters to your decision, ask about it before payment. Record the seller's relevant conditions and any restrictions associated with the purchasing service. A low price is less attractive if the item cannot meet a requirement you cannot verify in advance.

Superbuy's shopping-agent page warns that some second-hand or unverifiable third-party purchases are not eligible for returns or exchanges. Its 1688 guidance also describes different after-sales arrangements for different seller types. These examples are enough to show why a single rule should not be applied to every marketplace listing.

Do not infer eligibility from a recommendation page or a familiar product photograph. The seller, listing and selected service are the relevant context. If the conditions are unclear, ask Superbuy to clarify the specific order before proceeding.

## Identify the stage and the requested outcome

Start your enquiry by stating where the item is in the process. Then say what you want: clarification, correction of a purchasing option, cancellation if available, an exchange, or a return request. Those are different actions and should not be left for support to guess.

| Stage | Useful information to include | Question to resolve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before purchase | Listing, intended option and concern | Can the issue be clarified before payment? |
| Order in progress | Order reference and latest status | What action is still available at this stage? |
| In warehouse | Received-item evidence and selected option | Can the mismatch or defect be addressed? |
| Parcel submitted | Parcel reference and current status | Is any intervention still possible? |
| After delivery | Parcel evidence and item condition | Which after-sales procedure applies? |

Avoid promising yourself an outcome before it has been confirmed. A request submitted is not the same as a seller accepting it, and an accepted return is not the same as a completed refund.

## Describe the issue with observable evidence

Use a short comparison between what was ordered and what was received. For a wrong size, include the selected option and a readable label. For a missing component, identify the expected component and the evidence showing the contents. For visible damage, show its location in both an overview and a closer image when possible.

Separate an observed problem from an inference. “The left strap is detached at this point” is clearer than “the whole product is poor quality.” If you cannot tell whether a mark is a shadow or a defect, request a better view before presenting it as a confirmed defect.

Keep the original images as well as any annotated copies. An annotation can help direct attention, but an unedited overview preserves the context. Use the official order or after-sales channel rather than posting account details publicly.

## Preserve the item's condition while the case is open

Keep tags, accessories and packaging intact until you know what the applicable process requires. Do not request alteration, cleaning, label removal or disposal of packaging while deciding whether to return the item.

If you have already authorised a change, explain it honestly. Do not conceal a modification in the hope of making the case appear simpler. Accurate information helps establish which options remain available and avoids conflicting accounts later.

For an item still in the warehouse, do not include it in an international parcel while the issue is unresolved unless you have knowingly decided to keep it. The [warehouse planning guide](/articles/superbuy-warehouse-storage/) shows how to keep unresolved items separate from those ready to ship.

## Ask about costs and next steps together

Before agreeing to a return or exchange, ask what you need to do, which costs apply and what conditions must be met. For 1688 purchases, Superbuy's published guidance specifically notes seller-policy dependence and possible user responsibility for return or exchange shipping. Do not generalise that example into a fixed charge for every order.

Request an itemised explanation if a proposed settlement is unclear. Distinguish the product amount from domestic transport, international transport and optional services already performed. The word “refund” alone does not tell you which of those lines is included.

For an exchange, also clarify the replacement option and how its arrival will be handled. A replacement should go through its own identity and evidence check; it should not be assumed correct merely because it was sent after a complaint.

## Keep one coherent case record

Record the order reference, issue, requested outcome, evidence supplied and latest reply. Use dates so you can distinguish a current instruction from an earlier proposal. If you need to follow up, refer to the same case and explain what remains unanswered.

Avoid opening multiple contradictory requests for the same issue. If your preferred outcome changes, say so clearly in the existing conversation where possible. A concise update such as “I would now prefer the return option discussed in your last reply” is easier to act on than several competing messages.

Do not infer a resolution from silence. Check the status and ask for clarification when a required step or response is missing. Follow the applicable process promptly rather than relying on an assumed universal deadline from an old forum post.

## Reconcile the final outcome

Once an outcome is confirmed, compare it with the actual transaction record. Note the amount, currency, reference and destination of any credit or refund. If the record does not match the explanation you received, ask about that specific difference.

Keep a distinction between a credit recorded in an account and money received through a payment method. Check the relevant procedure and status rather than assuming those events occur at the same moment. We do not publish a fixed processing time because the applicable method and case need to be confirmed.

For a replacement, close the case only after checking the new item against the agreed option. For a partial resolution, record what was settled and what remains open so your budget and warehouse plan reflect the actual situation.

## Use the experience to improve the next order

Look for the information that would have prevented the uncertainty: a clearer size chart, a specific pre-purchase question, a better option record or a targeted warehouse photograph. Add that check to your process without assuming every future seller will behave the same way.

The most useful buying habit is to identify non-negotiable requirements early. If you cannot verify a requirement and cannot accept the applicable return conditions, choosing another item may be the more practical decision.
