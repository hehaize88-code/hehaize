Good packing begins with a clear description of what you want protected. “Make it cheap and safe” expresses a preference, but it does not tell a warehouse whether you care about an intact retail box, a rigid shape, delicate surfaces or minimum parcel dimensions.

This guide helps you turn those priorities into a practical packing brief. Superbuy offers packaging services and links to Shipping Expert from its calculator. Availability, service scope and charges must be checked in the current official flow; we do not assume that an advisory service includes every packing action or the international freight itself.

## A packing request you can adapt

Use this checklist to prepare an order-specific request. Replace each description with your actual item references and priorities, then confirm the available service and charge before authorising work.

| Include in the request | Your decision |
| --- | --- |
| Accepted items | List only items whose inspection is resolved |
| Packaging to keep | Identify boxes, tags or protection that must remain |
| Permitted changes | Specify what may be removed or folded |
| Protection needs | Identify fragile surfaces and shapes that must not be compressed |
| Cost approval | Ask for the service scope and charge before confirmation |
| Final comparison | Request the resulting packed weight and outer dimensions |

For shoes, use the [sneakers checklist](/articles/superbuy-sneakers-spreadsheet/) before deciding whether to keep the box. For clothing, use the [hoodie fit checklist](/articles/superbuy-hoodies-spreadsheet/) before approving compression or packaging removal. Resolve an uncertain fit or condition before making an irreversible packing change.

## Describe the contents before choosing a service

List the items in the intended parcel and note their vulnerabilities. Soft clothing may need a different arrangement from a structured hat, a rigid accessory or a fragile decorative object. A mixed parcel requires attention to how the items interact, not just how each would travel alone.

Identify surfaces that should not rub against one another and parts that should not carry weight. If the original packaging has a functional purpose, record it. If a box matters only because you want to keep it for display or storage, say that explicitly as a preference.

Keep shipping eligibility separate from protection. Strong packaging does not make a restricted item eligible for every route. Describe the contents accurately and check the applicable route conditions before deciding how to pack them.

## Resolve inspection and return questions first

Do not authorise a packaging change while you are still deciding whether the received item matches your order. Once a label is removed or a box is discarded, it may be difficult to restore the original condition for an investigation or return.

Review the [QC checklist](/articles/superbuy-qc-photos/) and give each item a clear decision. If you need a further measurement or seller response, keep that task open. An approaching shipping plan is not a reason to mark an unresolved item as accepted.

For an active return enquiry, ask what must remain intact. Keep the response with the order record. Packing instructions should follow the resolved decision about the item, rather than accidentally determining that decision for you.

## Rank your priorities

There are often several reasonable packing arrangements, and they can serve different goals. Write your priorities in order so a person helping with the parcel knows what matters most when a trade-off appears.

| Priority | Useful instruction | What still needs confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preserve a retail box | Keep this specified box intact | Suitable protection and final dimensions |
| Protect a rigid shape | Avoid compressing this item | Appropriate materials and space |
| Reduce avoidable bulk | Remove only the listed unnecessary packaging | Whether the remaining protection is adequate |
| Separate surfaces | Prevent these parts touching directly | Suitable wrapping arrangement |
| Stay within a budget | Ask before adding services beyond the limit | Actual service and shipping charges |

A precise priority is more actionable than an unlimited request for maximum protection. Additional material can change weight or dimensions, so ask how the proposed arrangement affects the parcel you plan to send.

## Evaluate packaging changes individually

Superbuy's service descriptions include options such as packaging removal, reinforcement, vacuum packing and folding shoe boxes. Their existence does not mean every option is appropriate for every item. Read the scope and assess the effect on your own contents.

For example, compression may be worth discussing for suitable soft goods, while it could conflict with your preference to preserve a structured shape. Folding a box may reduce space but can change the box's condition. These are decisions about the object you want to receive, not just the shipping bill.

Ask one concrete question for each proposed change: what will be removed or altered, what protection remains, and what result should I expect? If the answer is unclear, clarify it before authorising work that cannot easily be reversed.

## Prepare a brief for packing assistance

Whether you use an available expert service or ask an order-specific question, gather the information needed to understand your request. Include the destination, relevant item references, contents, current measurements if known, preferred timing and the priorities you have ranked.

A practical brief might say: “These three accepted items are intended for one parcel. Keep the boxed item in its original box; do not compress the structured item. The clothing packaging may be removed if appropriate. Please explain the recommended arrangement and any additional charges before I confirm.” This is an editorial example, not a standard instruction that suits all parcels.

Avoid giving incompatible requirements without a priority. Asking for the smallest possible package while requiring every outer carton to remain unchanged may leave no useful room for adjustment. State which requirement is essential and which is a preference.

## Compare the resulting parcel, not a guessed saving

After a proposal, compare the expected parcel measurements and eligible routes. A smaller outer dimension is relevant only within the route's actual billing rules. Do not assume a specific percentage saving from removing a box or using a particular packing service.

Use the [shipping calculator guide](/articles/superbuy-shipping-calculator/) to compare a baseline and a proposed arrangement. Keep the destination and contents consistent, and identify which measurements remain estimates. This makes the comparison traceable if the packed result differs later.

Include service charges in the comparison. A packing change that lowers one part of the shipping estimate may add a separate service cost. Your decision should consider the complete displayed cost and the protection outcome, not just one favourable number.

## Confirm the scope of any expert service

Read the current service description before payment. Identify whether you are buying advice, a specific packing task, a measurement or another defined result. Check what is excluded and how further work is authorised.

Do not confuse purchasing assistance for difficult-to-source products with assistance planning an outbound parcel. Similar English service labels appear on different official pages. Use the actual service page and task description to confirm which service you are selecting.

If the scope is unclear, ask using your parcel brief. The question should be easy to answer: “Does this charge cover the requested packing work, or will that be billed separately?” Keep the answer and the confirmed order together.

## Make the final submission check

Compare the selected items with your accepted-item list. Review the address, route, declared contents and any required information in the official submission flow. Check the agreed packing instructions and make sure no earlier draft instruction conflicts with them.

Save the parcel reference after submission. If you later ask about a packing detail or shipping adjustment, use that reference and the specific instruction involved. It is more useful than sending a general message about “my order” when several purchases are in progress.

On receipt, compare the actual arrangement with your brief and record what worked. This gives you a personal reference for future packing decisions while recognising that a different mix of items may require a different approach.
