Choosing a size through a shopping agent is easier when you compare measurements made in the same way. A familiar letter or shoe-size number can be a useful label, but it is not a substitute for the chart attached to the particular product you are buying.

This guide offers a measurement workflow for clothing and shoes discovered through a Superbuy spreadsheet. The measuring suggestions are independent practical advice. Superbuy's role is to purchase the selected option and provide the services available for your order; this site does not promise that any catalogue product will fit you.

## Start with an item you already know

Choose a garment you wear comfortably for the same purpose as the new item. A fitted shirt is not a useful reference for the shape of an oversized sweatshirt unless you deliberately account for the difference. Write down what you like about its fit and what you would change.

Place the garment flat without stretching it and measure the dimensions relevant to your decision. Use the same method each time. A reference sheet becomes useful through consistency, not through the number of measurements you collect.

Record the garment's construction and your fit preference alongside the numbers. Stretch, lining, thickness and intended layering can affect how you interpret a dimension. Two garments with the same flat chest width may still feel different in use.

## Distinguish body measurements from garment measurements

A seller chart may describe a person's body or the garment itself. Read its labels before comparing it with your reference. A body circumference and a flat garment width are not the same measurement, even if both are described casually as “chest.”

If the chart is unclear, ask what the numbers represent. Do not automatically double or halve a figure until you know the measuring convention. Check the unit as well: centimetres and inches must be converted consistently before comparison.

For a chart that includes a tolerance, keep that information with your notes. If your choice depends on a very narrow difference between two sizes, the uncertainty in the measurement may matter more than the size label itself.

## Use a small reference table

Choose the dimensions that control fit for the garment. The following table is a starting point for deciding what to record, not a universal sizing formula.

| Item | Useful comparison | Method to keep consistent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T-shirt or sweatshirt | Chest width and body length | Flat, unstretched, same seam reference points |
| Jacket | Chest, shoulder and sleeve | Account for intended layers and construction |
| Trousers | Waist, rise and inseam | Note whether the waistband is relaxed or stretched |
| Shorts | Waist, rise and leg opening | Compare with a similar cut |
| Structured headwear | Relevant internal dimensions | Clarify the seller's measurement method |

You do not need every possible measurement for every item. Focus on the dimensions that would make you choose a different size or reject the product altogether.

## Read the exact seller chart

Use the chart belonging to the current listing and variation. Do not assume that another seller's chart applies because the photographs look similar. If a listing contains several cuts or versions, confirm which chart corresponds to your selection.

Compare the candidate sizes side by side with your reference garment. Mark which dimensions are acceptable, uncertain or outside your preference. This makes the decision more explicit than choosing your usual letter and hoping the seller uses the same convention.

If the chart provides only height-and-weight suggestions, treat them as a limited starting point. People with the same height and weight can have different proportions and fit preferences. Ask for relevant garment measurements when the available information is insufficient for your decision.

## Approach shoe sizing as a separate comparison

For shoes, begin with the seller's stated sizing method and the particular model's information. A conversion between EU, UK and US labels is not a complete fit assessment. Length, width, shape and intended use all matter to the wearer.

If you measure your feet, use a repeatable method and record both feet. Compare those measurements with guidance that clearly states what its figures mean. A foot-length recommendation, an insole length and an outsole length describe different things and should not be substituted for one another.

When requesting a warehouse measurement, specify the relevant part and method. Ask whether the requested measurement can be taken reliably. A photograph of a tape against a curved exterior may not provide the internal fit information you actually need.

## Ask one precise pre-purchase question

If an uncertainty would change your size choice, resolve it before ordering where possible. Include the product link, intended option, the chart you are reading and the dimension that is unclear. A narrow question is easier to answer than “What size should I get?” without context.

For example, you might ask whether a listed waist measurement is a circumference or a flat width, or whether a sleeve measurement starts at the shoulder seam or the collar. The answer helps you make a consistent comparison with your own reference.

Do not use an order note to contradict the selected size. If you choose M in the form and ask for L in a comment, you have created a preventable ambiguity. Confirm the intended option in the order fields before paying.

## Verify the received item when it matters

After warehouse arrival, first check the size label and selected variation. If a critical measurement remains uncertain, request a targeted photograph or measurement through the relevant available service. Superbuy's service page describes optional detailed photography and inspection; confirm the current scope before purchasing extra work.

Specify the endpoints, the unit and whether the item should be relaxed or stretched. For example: “Please show the flat, unstretched width between the underarm seams, with the tape and both endpoints visible.” This is more useful than asking for a general photo of the garment on a table.

Review the result against the reference you prepared before buying. If it falls outside your acceptable range, investigate the available options before international shipment. The [returns guide](/articles/superbuy-returns/) explains how to keep a request tied to the order and evidence.

## Make a decision with uncertainty visible

Measurements reduce uncertainty; they do not remove it completely. Fabric behaviour, construction and personal preference still affect fit. Avoid describing a size as guaranteed when your evidence supports only a reasonable comparison.

Write a short decision note: the selected size, the relevant measurements and any remaining uncertainty you accept. This can help you learn from the fit after delivery without relying on memory or a size label that means little outside that listing.

For a costly item or one needed for a specific occasion, decide whether the available evidence is enough before committing. If you cannot verify the necessary dimensions and cannot accept the return conditions, another product may be a better choice.

## Improve your reference after delivery

Try the item in its intended use and compare the result with your pre-purchase notes. Record what worked and which dimension you would change next time. Keep any observations about washing or wear separate from the original warehouse measurement.

Over time, your reference becomes more useful than a generic instruction to size up or down. It remains tied to measurable features and your own preferences, which is the information you need when comparing the next listing in the [spreadsheet](/spreadsheet/).
