A warehouse lets you separate buying from international shipping. That flexibility is useful when orders arrive at different times, but it also creates a planning problem: one slow item can keep an otherwise ready parcel waiting while older items approach their storage limits.

The solution is a small arrival-and-decision record. Track each item's storage status, inspection outcome and intended parcel group. Then make a deliberate choice about waiting, splitting or shipping, using the current information in your Superbuy account.

## Know which storage terms you are relying on

At our review on 19 October 2026, Superbuy's fee page stated a 90-day free storage period, followed by a daily storage charge, with a normal maximum storage period of 180 days. The page also described a paid extension of the maximum period. Check the current item record and terms before relying on a deadline or extension.

These periods should not be treated as one interchangeable allowance. The end of free storage and the maximum storage limit answer different questions. A paid extension of the maximum period does not necessarily restart free storage; the official description says daily charges continue after the free period ends.

Do not calculate a deadline solely from the day you paid the seller. Use the arrival and storage information recorded for that item. If the displayed dates are unclear, ask support before the deadline becomes urgent.

Set your own reminder earlier than the displayed deadline, allowing time to ask questions and make a decision. A reminder is a planning aid; it does not change the official storage period or replace checking your account.

## Keep an item-level warehouse record

A parcel-level note saying “all orders should arrive soon” hides the differences between items. Give each order its own status, even if you expect to ship them together.

| Field | Example of what to record |
| --- | --- |
| Order reference | The reference shown in your account |
| Warehouse status | Awaiting arrival, available for review or ready |
| Arrival information | Recorded date and displayed storage deadline |
| Inspection decision | Accepted, needs evidence or issue raised |
| Parcel group | Intended grouping, still provisional |
| Next action | Request a measurement, ask about delay or submit |
| Review date | When you will make the next decision |

Keep the record short and update it when the situation changes. Its purpose is to make an action visible, not to duplicate every field in your account.

## Separate arrival from readiness

An item can be in the warehouse without being ready for your parcel. You may still need a measurement, a missing component confirmed or a seller response about a mismatch. Mark that distinction explicitly.

Before placing an item in the ready group, review the evidence relevant to your purchase. A rushed decision caused by another item's delay can undermine the benefit of warehouse inspection. The [QC guide](/articles/superbuy-qc-photos/) provides a review sequence you can apply consistently.

If an issue is unresolved, keep the next action specific. “Waiting” is not enough to tell whether anyone needs to do something. “Awaiting a photo of the size label requested through order X” gives you a meaningful basis for a follow-up.

## Give consolidation a purpose

Combining items into a parcel can simplify delivery planning. Superbuy's fee page describes consolidation, but whether a particular grouping is suitable or economical depends on the resulting parcel and available routes. Do not assume that the largest possible combination is the best one.

Group items according to compatibility, protection requirements and timing. Soft clothing may pack differently from a rigid collectible box or a fragile component. An item with a particular shipping restriction can also change which options apply to the group.

Evaluate the combination after considering the actual contents. If one item makes the intended route unavailable, investigate an appropriate separate arrangement instead of changing its description or omitting it from the declared contents.

## Set a decision date for late arrivals

Choose a date when you will decide whether the ready items should leave without the delayed one. Base it on your needs, the oldest stored item and the uncertainty around the delay. This is a personal planning date, not a promise that the seller or carrier will complete anything by then.

For example, imagine three items are accepted after review and a fourth has no confirmed seller dispatch. You can compare waiting with sending the three ready items and considering the fourth later. The relevant trade-off includes additional parcel costs, storage exposure and your need for the items, not just the appeal of one shipment.

Write down what new information would change your decision. A confirmed dispatch update may justify a short wait; a vague expectation may not. This keeps the decision responsive to evidence rather than repeatedly postponing it.

## Compare a combined parcel with a split

Prepare a complete set of inputs for each scenario. A combined parcel has one packed shape; two separate parcels each have their own packaging and applicable charges. You cannot compare them accurately by dividing one estimate in half.

Use the official calculator with the relevant destination, weight, dimensions and item categories. Keep the estimates dated and mark uncertain measurements. Our [shipping calculator guide](/articles/superbuy-shipping-calculator/) explains how to avoid changing several unrelated assumptions at once.

Consider convenience as well as cost. A split may mean tracking two deliveries and keeping two parcel records. A combined shipment may mean waiting longer. Neither is universally preferable; the right choice depends on the actual constraints and your priorities.

## Address storage limits before they become urgent

Review the oldest items first. If you need more time, investigate the applicable extension option while there is still time to clarify its scope. Do not assume that paying a storage charge automatically extends every maximum limit or that an unanswered enquiry suspends a deadline.

Ask support to confirm the specific item, applicable deadline and action required. Keep the reply with your record. If you no longer want an item, ask about the available options rather than leaving it indefinitely with no decision.

Storage is also a budget line. Add confirmed storage or extension charges to the relevant order or parcel record so they are not forgotten when you calculate the complete cost. The [fees guide](/articles/superbuy-fees/) shows a simple way to keep confirmed and estimated amounts separate.

## Submit only the group you have reviewed

Before parcel submission, compare the selected items with your ready list. Check quantities and make sure an unresolved order has not been included accidentally. Then review the intended packaging and route conditions for that exact group.

After submission, keep the parcel reference alongside the item references. This creates a clear connection between the purchasing stage and the delivery stage. If you later need help, you can identify whether the question concerns a seller order, warehouse service or outbound parcel.

The purpose of warehouse planning is not to hold items as long as possible. It is to use the available time for inspection and sensible grouping, then move each accepted item toward delivery with a clear record of the decisions you made.
