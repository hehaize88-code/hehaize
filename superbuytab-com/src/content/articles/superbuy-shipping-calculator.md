To estimate Superbuy shipping, open the official calculator, select the destination and warehouse, enter weight in grams and outer parcel dimensions in centimetres, then choose the actual item category. Compare the eligible routes using those same inputs and save the result with the date and measurements.

The calculator produces an estimate for the parcel you describe. It does not establish a universal price per kilogram or guarantee the final shipping charge. If a result looks unexpectedly high, check the dimensions and billing rule using our [shipping cost troubleshooting guide](/articles/why-superbuy-shipping-expensive/).

## Quick checklist before requesting a quote

Keep the destination, warehouse, item categories, physical weight and outer parcel dimensions together. Mark every unconfirmed measurement as an estimate. Save the route name, billing rule, displayed charge and date with each result so you can reproduce the comparison later.

| Before comparing | Before submitting | After the final charge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Check units and actual contents | Confirm packed measurements and route eligibility | Reconcile the payment with the final bill |
| Record required packaging | Read included services and exclusions | Keep the parcel and payment references |

For destination-specific checks, use the [USA shipping guide](/articles/superbuy-shipping-to-usa/) or [UK shipping guide](/articles/superbuy-shipping-to-uk/). These explain how to separate transport from destination charges. A calculator result alone does not establish the applicable import treatment.

## Begin with a parcel description

Write down what you expect to send, where it needs to go and which packaging must be retained. Include features relevant to shipping eligibility, such as batteries or liquids, when they are present. Accurate category information is part of obtaining a useful estimate; it is not an optional detail to change until a cheaper route appears.

Separate known measurements from guesses. If the items have not reached the warehouse, label the weight and dimensions as provisional. A seller's product dimensions may describe the object, not the packed parcel. Likewise, an item's weight may exclude the protection and outer packaging needed for shipment.

Save a first estimate as a budget scenario. Update it when better measurements become available instead of treating it as a commitment by the carrier or Superbuy.

## Enter units carefully

The official calculator displays weight in grams and dimensions in centimetres. Convert your measurements before entering them. Two kilograms is 2,000 grams; entering 2 in a grams field describes a very different parcel.

Keep the length, width and height of the packed outer parcel together. Do not mix a product measurement from one source with a carton measurement from another. If you are comparing packaging options, record a complete set of dimensions for each option.

Before searching, read every field once as a sentence: “This parcel goes to this destination, from this warehouse, weighs this amount, measures these dimensions and contains these item categories.” That simple check often reveals an incorrect unit or an old destination left from a previous estimate.

## Distinguish physical weight from volumetric weight

Physical weight comes from a scale. Volumetric weight represents the space a package occupies under a route's calculation rule. The calculator's explanation gives an example divisor of 6,000 for air cargo; that example should not be assumed to apply unchanged to every available route.

For an arithmetic illustration only, a parcel measuring 40 × 30 × 20 cm has a volume of 24,000 cm³. With a divisor of 6,000, that produces a volumetric figure of 4 kg. This is not a shipping quote and does not establish which weight a particular route will charge.

Read the route's own billing rules, including its divisor, rounding and any size limits. The calculator inputs describe the parcel; the route conditions explain how that description becomes a charge.

## Compare scenarios with one change at a time

Start with a baseline that reflects your current plan. Then change one meaningful variable, such as keeping a bulky retail box versus an approved smaller packing arrangement. If you change the destination, weight, dimensions and contents at once, you cannot tell which change explains the difference in results.

| Scenario | Keep fixed | Change | Decision it helps answer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Packaging comparison | Contents and destination | Confirmed outer dimensions | Is the packaging change worth considering? |
| Parcel split | Destination and total contents | Grouping and each parcel's measurements | Does a split meet practical and route constraints? |
| Route comparison | Same complete parcel inputs | Available route | Which conditions suit this shipment? |
| Estimate update | Intended contents and packing | Better measured data | Is the earlier budget still realistic? |

Do not replace a real constraint with an optimistic assumption just to improve the estimate. If protective packaging is required, include it in the scenario you intend to use.

## Read beyond the headline price

A route result should be reviewed for eligibility and conditions as well as cost. Look for weight and dimension limits, supported item categories, the meaning of any estimated delivery range and the relevant service terms. An attractive price is not useful if the parcel does not qualify.

The official calculator explains that different delivery methods have their own weight limits. If a parcel exceeds a route's limits, changing the input to a lower number does not solve the physical problem. Reassess the parcel composition or ask for an appropriate shipping option.

Keep route names and the date with your saved comparison. Availability and quotations can change, so a screenshot or note from an earlier planning session is a record of that session, not a permanent entitlement to the same result.

## Evaluate packaging through both cost and protection

A smaller parcel can be attractive when space affects the charge, but smaller is not automatically suitable. A fragile item, a structured bag and folded clothing have different vulnerabilities. Identify the protection the item needs before evaluating a proposed reduction.

Ask what will be removed, what will remain and whether the change is reversible. If a retail box matters to you, say so. If it is part of a pending return investigation, resolve that issue before authorising removal.

Use our [packing guide](/articles/superbuy-packing/) to prepare an explicit request. A clear instruction helps the warehouse understand your priorities without having to guess whether you value minimum dimensions, an intact retail box or extra cushioning most.

## Understand the difference between an estimate and settlement

Superbuy's fee explanation describes an initial international shipping payment based on estimated parcel information, followed by reconciliation against the logistics provider's bill. This is why a calculator result should remain labelled as an estimate in your budget.

When you submit a parcel, keep the submitted route, packing choices and payment reference. If a later adjustment appears, compare it with the final parcel information and the relevant bill rather than with an unrelated estimate saved weeks earlier.

If you cannot reconcile a difference, ask support for an itemised explanation using the parcel reference. A useful enquiry identifies the original estimate, the final charge and the specific input or fee line you do not understand.

## Make a final comparison you can act on

Choose among options that genuinely apply to your parcel. Compare total displayed charges and service conditions, then check whether the delivery plan fits your budget and practical deadline. A delivery estimate should not be treated as a guaranteed arrival date for an event you cannot miss.

Keep a fallback plan if the final measurements make your preferred route unavailable. That might mean changing the grouping, postponing a nonessential item or seeking advice on another eligible service. Decide based on confirmed information rather than forcing the parcel to resemble your first estimate.
