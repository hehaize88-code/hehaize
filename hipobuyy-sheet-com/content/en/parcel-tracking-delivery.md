## Choose the correct record to track

A China shopping-agent purchase crosses at least two delivery stages. The seller sends an item to the agent's warehouse, and the agent later sends a selected parcel internationally. Tracking one does not tell you the state of the other. Hipobuy's official Order Status Display makes this distinction: Shipped (Local) is the seller's domestic movement, while Shipped (International) belongs to a parcel submitted for overseas delivery. Save both the item order number and the parcel number, and do not treat an old local scan as the latest event for the international package.

Before either tracking number exists, verify the exact seller listing and selected variant. An agent order can be pending payment, awaiting processing or already purchased without being physically dispatched by the seller. The official status guide also lists Outstanding Payment when the product or domestic delivery costs more than the initial order amount. If a buyer has not completed that stage, waiting for a courier scan is premature. Read the status and the line item before asking where the parcel is.

## From seller dispatch to warehouse review

Shipped (Local) says the seller has sent the goods toward the Hipobuy warehouse. The platform's guide mentions a normal arrival range for this leg, but it is not a promise for your seller or product. Arrived means the warehouse has received the order and is waiting for inspection. Stored means it is warehoused, and Hipobuy advertises 90 days of free storage for that status. None of those labels says an international carrier has collected your parcel.

When an item reaches the warehouse, review its QC photos against the chosen size, colour and model. If you intend to return an eligible product, the official returns page uses a separate five-day application period after Warehoused. Waiting for international tracking to appear before examining QC would miss the important earlier decision. Keep the seller's local tracking and agent status in the order folder, then mark whether the item is approved to join a parcel.

| Record | Event | What it does not prove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seller order | Shipped (Local) | Warehouse inspection is finished |
| Warehouse item | Arrived | It is approved for overseas shipping |
| Stored item | QC and storage | International carrier has collected it |
| Submitted parcel | Waiting for Packing | The final packed amount is settled |
| International parcel | Shipped (International) | Customs and final delivery are complete |

## Packing and carrier collection are separate moments

Once selected items become a submitted parcel, the official guide describes a payment step and then Waiting for Packing. At this stage the warehouse is packing and weighing the goods and preparing the parcel for logistics collection. Waiting for Confirmation can mean that the actual package weight is higher than the estimated weight and more shipping payment is required. If this appears, compare the final weight, route and amount before assuming tracking has stalled. A parcel awaiting a necessary confirmation has not simply disappeared in transit.

Waiting for Delivery means outbound processing is complete but the package is awaiting pickup by the logistics provider. Shipped (International) means the parcel has been sent abroad. An online tracking number may take time to show the carrier's first event, and the timing of public scans can differ by provider. The official page does not promise a fixed interval between those labels. Record the platform's current status and any carrier events with their timestamps, then use the route's stated contact process if they remain inconsistent.

## Keep a timeline that support can use

A useful tracking note has four fields: date and time, source of the update, exact status text and what action you took. Include whether the update came from the Hipobuy order page, the Hipobuy parcel page or the carrier. A screenshot of an isolated word such as “shipped” is ambiguous; its record and date matter. When contacting support, state the order or parcel ID and the last confirmed event. Avoid asking a seller to explain an international parcel event, or a carrier to resolve an unpurchased item.

Suppose two jackets were ordered separately and placed in one parcel. One seller order may show Stored for weeks and the other for only a few days. The new parcel's tracking number covers the combined shipment; it does not erase the two original seller records. If the delivered box seems to lack one jacket, compare its contents with the parcel manifest and the two item IDs. That is a better starting point than the phrase “my order is missing.”

## Delivery is where photographs can matter again

Hipobuy's Receipt Information notice advises checking the external packaging, seal and apparent weight at delivery and inspecting the goods with the courier where possible. It describes keeping the outer box and sealing material and promptly providing invoice and photos if a parcel is damaged or incomplete. Its separate after-sales guidance gives examples of useful evidence for wrong size, wrong style, missing goods and weight discrepancy. Read the current notice for your precise deadline rather than assuming every issue has the same one.

If the carton looks compromised, photograph it before discarding packaging. Keep the shipping label and a view of the opened contents, recording any mismatch with the parcel manifest. Do not manipulate a package to manufacture a claim. Some of the platform's compensation exclusions relate to missing proof or an incident occurring after normal delivery. Accurate, timely evidence can help a real case; it does not guarantee a successful claim.

## When a tracking update stops

A quiet tracking page can mean several things: collection has not yet produced a scan, a carrier handoff has occurred without a public update, or a process is genuinely delayed. The status labels alone do not identify which explanation is true. First confirm the parcel is past payment and packing. Then compare its current platform status with the selected carrier's official events. If the discrepancy persists beyond the route's stated expectation, use the parcel ID, route and timestamps in a support request. Do not copy a confident delivery estimate from a different country or season.

The international parcel agreement says rates and timelines may be adjusted. Customs and taxation notices describe possible destination inspections. These are further reasons not to turn a single buyer's timeline into a promise. If a shipment ultimately arrives, record the actual dates alongside the destination and route when writing about it; that gives future readers context.

A tracking guide succeeds when it tells you which organization and record can answer the next question. Seller-to-warehouse, warehouse-to-carrier and carrier-to-recipient are distinct handoffs. Check the exact event, preserve the matching ID and document the state of the box on arrival. That small habit is more useful than refreshing a generic tracking page without knowing which parcel it represents.


## Leave a useful handoff note

If another person will receive the parcel, share the tracking number, expected carrier and what to check on the outside of the box, without sending unnecessary account access. Ask them to keep the packaging if damage is visible and to tell you the delivery time. That helps connect the final carrier event to the received contents.
