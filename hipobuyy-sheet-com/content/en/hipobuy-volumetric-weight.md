Hipobuy shipping estimates can change when a light item occupies a large box. Compare packed actual weight with the volumetric calculation required by the selected route, then apply that route's rounding and pricing rules. The examples below explain the arithmetic; their divisors and amounts are illustrative, not current Hipobuy tariffs.

## Start with the outside of the packed parcel

Product weight, warehouse item weight and final parcel weight are different records. A seller's garment weight may exclude a shoe box, outer carton, padding and other packing materials. Dimensions matter after those choices are made. Measuring the folded garment alone will not establish the space occupied by its final shipping box. Save the packed weight and the outside length, width and height together so that comparisons refer to one configuration.

Hipobuy's [official estimator](https://hipobuy.com/estimation) asks for destination, weight and product type and offers dimensions. Enter the units shown by that form; do not paste kilograms into a field labelled grams. If you only have estimated measurements, mark them as estimates and replace them after packing. Our [shipping calculator](/en/articles/hipobuy-shipping-calculator/) uses kilograms and centimetres, so it is particularly important to check units when moving between tools.

## Calculate volumetric weight using the route's divisor

When a route uses this method, the usual calculation is outside length × width × height divided by its stated divisor, using the units specified in the rule. The divisor is not a universal property of Hipobuy. Read the eligible route's current conditions instead of assuming a number from a different courier, country or old screenshot. A mathematically correct result with the wrong divisor is still the wrong planning input.

Consider an illustrative 40 × 30 × 30 cm parcel weighing 2 kg. Its volume is 36,000 cubic centimetres. With an example divisor of 6,000, volumetric weight is 6 kg. If the route uses the greater of actual and volumetric weight, the pre-rounding basis would be 6 kg. This demonstrates why a light parcel can have a higher billed weight; it does not establish that any particular live route uses this divisor or billing method.

## Keep the billing basis and rounding separate

A route may have its own basis, increments, minimums or size limits. First identify the weight basis, then apply its rounding rule, and only then apply its price structure. Do not round a dimension or intermediate result down simply because a lower number is more convenient. If the route gives specific measurement or rounding instructions, those instructions control the calculation. Keep a copy of the conditions with the quote.

| Illustrative input | Calculation | Planning result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 kg, 40 × 30 × 30 cm, divisor 6,000 | 36,000 ÷ 6,000 | 6 kg volumetric |
| Greater-of-two basis | Maximum of 2 and 6 | 6 kg before rounding |
| 2 kg, 40 × 30 × 20 cm, same divisor | 24,000 ÷ 6,000 | 4 kg volumetric |
| 4.01 kg basis, hypothetical 0.5 kg increment | Round upward to next increment | 4.5 kg billed |

## Compare packing changes without promising savings

In the example, reducing the height from 30 to 20 cm changes volumetric weight from 6 to 4 kg while the other dimensions stay the same. That is a useful comparison only if the item can be packed safely that way and the relevant route uses the assumed method. Removing a protective box may reduce space but also alter protection, return options or the condition in which an item arrives. Keep those tradeoffs visible.

Ask what packaging would be removed, what protection would remain and whether a new measurement is available. A request to remove all packaging is less precise than identifying an unnecessary outer box while retaining protection around a fragile component. Do not assume compression is appropriate for every material or construction. The [rehearsal and packing guide](/en/articles/hipobuy-rehearsal-packing/) covers the information to request before deciding on a final parcel configuration.

## Compare one combined parcel with separate parcels

Consolidation can reduce duplicated packaging, but a larger combined box can change size limits, weight tiers or eligible routes. Compare complete alternatives using the same items and destination. One parcel requires one packed quote; two parcels require two quotes and two sets of relevant fees. Adding item weights without accounting for the separate outer packaging does not produce a fair comparison. Record the contents of each option explicitly.

For a hypothetical comparison, call the combined configuration A and the separate configurations B1 and B2. Compare A's final total with B1 plus B2, including the services you actually need. If a route is unavailable for one configuration, mark it unavailable rather than filling the gap with a guessed price. A lower theoretical billed weight is not useful when the package fails the selected route's size or contents requirements.

## Distinguish a weight model from a shipping quote

The calculator on this site can compare actual and volumetric weight and apply an entered rounding increment. Its optional cost model is fixed fee plus billed kilograms multiplied by an entered USD rate. That model does not reproduce every shipping tariff. A first-weight charge, subsequent-weight tiers, minimum payment, remote-area charge or other service condition can make a live quote behave differently. The result is planning arithmetic, not a payment request.

Leave the rate blank when you do not have a comparable current rate. A weight result without a price is more useful than a precise-looking cost built from an invented tariff. If you enter a rate, preserve the date, route, destination and conditions from which it came. Currency conversion and charges outside the model need separate treatment. Use the [full cost checklist](/en/articles/shipping-cost-checklist/) to retain those additional budget lines.

## Recheck a quote that changes after packing

Compare the original estimate with the packed record field by field: actual weight, each dimension, billing basis, divisor, rounding, eligible route and selected paid services. Change one input at a time in your own comparison. This helps identify whether the difference comes from packaging, a different route or an extra service. A final amount alone does not explain the cause, and a discrepancy does not automatically demonstrate an error.

Hipobuy's published parcel status guidance says Waiting for Confirmation can require additional shipping payment when actual weight exceeds the estimate. Check the explanation on your own parcel rather than assuming all adjustments have that cause. If the calculation remains unclear, request the packed measurements and the applicable billing rule before approval. Retain the answer with the quote. Do not infer a fixed processing time or guaranteed reduction from a request for clarification.

## Save a reusable parcel comparison record

Your record should contain the selected items, destination, date, packed actual weight, outside dimensions, route name, billing basis, divisor where applicable, rounding, price structure and final payable amount. Add the packaging decision and any service exclusions that mattered. Keep hypothetical planning inputs separate from the measurements confirmed by the warehouse. This creates a useful reference for your next parcel without presenting one shipment as a permanent price list.

Before payment, verify that the route is still offered for the actual contents and destination, that all items have completed your QC decision and that the current quote matches the packing you approved. Recalculate if the contents or box change. The practical aim is a traceable comparison: you should be able to explain why one configuration costs more, what protection it retains and which numbers remain estimates until the final parcel is ready.
