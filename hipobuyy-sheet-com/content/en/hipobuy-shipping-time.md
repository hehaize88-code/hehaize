Hipobuy shipping time has several separate clocks: seller dispatch, delivery to the warehouse, inspection, parcel preparation and international transport. A route’s delivery estimate does not automatically cover all of them. Start with the current order or parcel status, then count from the event that actually starts that stage.

If you need a parcel for a particular date, check stock and seller dispatch before buying, then review the eligible international route after packing. This guide provides a planning method, not a universal delivery promise. For a parcel that has already stopped updating, use the [tracking troubleshooting guide](/en/articles/hipobuy-tracking-not-updating/).

## How long does Hipobuy take?

There is no single reliable order-to-door number for every purchase. An in-stock item dispatched promptly and an item awaiting production begin their journeys differently. Several sellers in one basket can also produce several arrival dates. The slowest item matters if you choose to wait and combine everything into one parcel.

The official status notice describes the seller-to-warehouse leg after local dispatch as normally taking three to five days. That statement concerns local delivery to the warehouse, not international delivery to your address. It also does not include the seller’s wait before dispatch. Check the live record when deciding whether a particular order needs attention.

For international transport, read the currently available route’s estimate and its starting event. Ask whether the range uses working days or calendar days and whether collection, clearance and final delivery are included. A range without those definitions is difficult to compare with another service.

## Identify the stage before counting days

Keep the item order and international parcel record separate. An item can be ready in the warehouse while no international parcel has yet been submitted. Likewise, creating a parcel or receiving a tracking number is not the same evidence as a carrier collection scan.

| Stage | Useful evidence | What to check next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seller preparation | Purchase confirmation and dispatch message | Stock, variant and seller dispatch commitment |
| Domestic transport | Local tracking and warehouse receipt | Whether the seller’s shipment reached the warehouse |
| Warehouse processing | Item status, inspection result and photos | Missing views, measurements or unresolved item issues |
| Parcel preparation | Parcel number, packing and payment record | Required confirmation and final packed quote |
| International transport | Named carrier and dated scans | Route estimate, handoff and delivery instructions |

Save the date and time of each completed handoff. This gives you a useful timeline without treating a guessed arrival date as fact. If the interface and carrier show different time zones, keep both original timestamps instead of silently subtracting one from the other.

## Seller dispatch and warehouse arrival

Before paying, verify the exact colour, size and version and ask about stock when the listing is unclear. A catalogue entry shows an item to investigate; it does not establish that every option can ship today. Customisation and preorders need their own dispatch expectations.

After a seller dispatches, consult the local shipment associated with that item. Do not enter a domestic tracking number into an unrelated international carrier and interpret the empty result as a lost parcel. If the seller has not dispatched, a question about stock or dispatch is more useful than a question about international shipping speed.

When several items are involved, maintain one line per item: purchased date, seller dispatch, warehouse arrival and whether it is needed in the first parcel. This lets you decide whether waiting for one late item is worth delaying the rest. Splitting can change total shipping cost, so compare the cost and timing together.

## Inspection, QC and your own decisions

Hipobuy distinguishes arrival awaiting inspection from storage in its status guide. Arrival therefore does not establish that the full QC photo set is already ready. Inspect the actual order’s photos when available and check size, colour, quantity and any measurement needed for your decision.

Request a specific missing view instead of repeatedly asking for “better QC.” A flat chest measurement or a clear size label gives the warehouse a concrete task. Keep any return deadline in view while waiting; a photo request is not an automatic extension. The [missing QC photos guide](/en/articles/hipobuy-qc-photos-not-showing/) explains how to separate processing from a display problem.

Time spent waiting for another purchase, approving a measurement or choosing packaging belongs in your own plan. It is not carrier transit time. Record such decisions separately so that a later shipping review explains what actually happened rather than blaming every elapsed day on the delivery line.

## Packing, payment and carrier collection

Before submitting the parcel, confirm its contents, destination, protection and eligible shipping options. The final packed measurements may differ from the earlier estimate. An outstanding confirmation or payment request can require action before the next handoff; check the parcel’s own message instead of assuming passive waiting will resolve it.

The official parcel workflow separates preparation, readiness for collection and international dispatch. A tracking label alone should not be used to prove physical collection. Look for the event description and ask support to clarify the handoff if it remains ambiguous.

Keep your selected route, quote and packed weight together. If you change packaging or divide the shipment, check the new quote and timeline. A cheaper route or smaller package does not automatically mean earlier arrival. Use the [shipping calculator](/en/articles/hipobuy-shipping-calculator/) for weight scenarios, then rely on the actual eligible quote for payment.

## International transport and the last mile

Follow the carrier named in the parcel record. A route can involve more than one operator; a local delivery number may appear after a handoff. Use a newly supplied number only when the parcel record or carrier connects it to your shipment. Save both references if they describe different legs.

A gap between visible scans does not by itself prove that movement has stopped. Equally, it is not evidence that arrival is imminent. Read the last event literally, compare it with the current route’s stated range and check for a request requiring your response. Avoid assigning a fixed waiting period to every country or route.

After a delivery attempt, follow the actual carrier’s collection or redelivery instructions promptly. Confirm the address and contact information before shipment to reduce avoidable problems. If the record says delivered but the parcel is missing, check the delivery evidence and contact the relevant official support channels with the parcel reference.

## Build a realistic arrival plan

Work backwards from your preferred date, but keep estimates as ranges. List what is known, what remains unknown and which event will update the estimate. An unconfirmed seller dispatch should remain an uncertainty rather than being replaced by the fastest experience found in a forum.

Consider an illustrative planning exercise: one item is ready in storage and another has not left its seller. Buying a faster international service cannot remove the second seller’s preparation time. You could wait, remove that item from the first parcel, or compare separate shipments. None of those options has a guaranteed delivery date until the actual conditions are known.

For a fixed event, leave room for an unresolved inspection, packing change or transport exception. Do not buy on the assumption that every stage will match its shortest possible duration. If a deadline is essential and the route cannot support it, reconsider the purchase before committing further costs.

## What to send support when a stage takes longer

Prepare a compact message containing the relevant item or parcel number, selected route where applicable, last dated status and the action you need clarified. Ask one concrete question: whether the seller dispatched, whether inspection is complete, whether confirmation is outstanding, or whether the carrier collected the parcel.

Attach relevant screenshots privately through the official service. Do not post addresses, phone numbers or complete tracking records in public comments. Keep one thread and add new evidence there so that the history remains understandable. Follow any stated investigation or claim deadline instead of assuming there is an unlimited period to report a problem.

For your own future planning, record seller dispatch, warehouse readiness, collection and delivery separately. Compare later shipments with the same destination, route and stage definitions. A clean timeline is much more useful than a single “took X days” claim with no starting event.
