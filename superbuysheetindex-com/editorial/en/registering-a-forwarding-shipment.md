# Registering a Superbuy forwarding shipment before your seller sends it
The easiest time to connect a self-purchased item with its warehouse record is before the seller prints a label. You can still change an address, ask what the box will contain and correct a description without having to trace a parcel in transit. Use Superbuy's forwarding guide alongside the instructions in your own account. The sequence matters more than the position of any individual button.

## Make an accurate description from the seller order
Open the original purchase confirmation. Write down the merchant, product name, chosen option, number of units and item type. Use words that distinguish the actual thing inside the box. “Case” could describe a suitcase, a phone cover or a protective box for a camera. “Fabric camera pouch, small, empty” communicates more to someone reviewing what is being sent. Be equally precise when equipment includes batteries, accessories or detachable components.

For a set, distinguish the unit sold from the number of physical pieces. One table-tennis set might contain two paddles and several balls. One seller order for two such sets is two ordered units with several components inside. Tell the warehouse how the seller describes the quantity and keep a separate components note if a missing piece would matter. A catalogue photograph of two paddles is insufficient on its own to establish what the selected option includes.

## Use the form that corresponds to your own shipment
Superbuy describes forwarding submissions for an already prepared parcel and for products associated with an online listing. Follow the option that fits what you actually have. If a relative is sending a used item, do not paste a different retailer's link simply to make the fields look complete. Describe the physical item and consult the account form or support about required information.

A listing link is helpful when it matches the item. Save the option as well as the general address. A seller can edit a listing after your purchase, or one listing can combine a base model with a more elaborate option. The person reviewing a forwarding request may see the current page; your original confirmation records what you bought. Keep the two together and indicate any difference that affects what should arrive.

## Check the selected receiving warehouse
Use the warehouse shown for your request and the complete receiving instructions associated with your account. A warehouse photograph on a forum, an address from a previous order and a friend's saved checkout address are poor substitutes. Superbuy's guide explains that a unique identifier at the end of a user's address can help identify a parcel. Treat it as part of the address when preparing the seller's delivery details.

Consider a marketplace form that splits the address into region, city, district, street and extra details. Copying a whole address into the street field can duplicate the district while omitting the final identifier. After saving, read the address the way it will print on the label. Compare each part with the Superbuy address from top to bottom. If the seller must enter the address manually, ask them to confirm the completed destination before handing the box to a carrier.

## Obtain the review result before promising dispatch
The official forwarding process puts review before sending the parcel. Wait for the result attached to the actual request and follow the current instructions or deadline shown there. A product of a similar type having passed previously does not establish whether this shipment is acceptable. The item, origin, warehouse and planned service can differ. If the review requests more information, answer the question as asked.

This is important if a merchant offers rapid dispatch. Tell the seller when you expect to provide the approved destination instead of guessing an address to satisfy a checkout countdown. If the seller has already dispatched the goods, do not describe them as still awaiting shipment. Record the time, address used and waybill; ask Superbuy which steps apply to an inbound parcel that has already left. A second submission cannot physically redirect it.

## Add the domestic carrier and the right number
When the merchant issues the shipping notice, copy the carrier name and its waybill. Keep the original string of characters, including leading zeroes. A marketplace purchase number can appear near the carrier number and look equally plausible. Test your interpretation by opening the tracking detail associated with that particular shipping notice. The number should correspond to that delivery leg.

If a shop first creates one label and later replaces it, keep both notices but mark which label was actually used. Ask the seller which carrier collected the box. An old label may remain on an invoice even when no parcel moved under it. Update the forwarding logistics information as the account permits; if a field is locked, provide the corrected reference through official support.

## Prepare for sellers who split a delivery
A request describing three units does not guarantee one carton. A merchant may send what is available first and dispatch the remainder separately. Ask for a list matching pieces to carrier numbers. If there are two boxes, label them in your notes by their actual waybills. Avoid calling one “the second parcel” without a number, because the second box sent can arrive first.

Imagine a lamp body and shade purchased together. The seller packs the metal base in a small carton and the fragile shade in a larger one. The carrier delivers the base on Monday and the shade on Wednesday. You can reconcile that outcome if your notes identify each box and its expected contents. A single row stating “lamp delivered Monday” makes Wednesday's box look unexpected and Monday's receipt look complete. This example illustrates record keeping; check which information the current forwarding form needs for each waybill.

## Confirm intake without assuming the contents
Look at the warehouse record after the domestic carrier reports delivery. The delivery scan identifies a carrier event. A stocked entry identifies goods entered in the warehouse system. Compare the reference, name, quantity and any available photographs. If the incoming parcel contained two items, check both. A photograph of an opened carton can establish that something arrived without settling whether the full order is present.

If the entry differs from your forwarding description, preserve the original request and ask about the specific mismatch. For example, “The second waybill was expected to contain one white shade; the warehouse entry shows a metal base. Can you match the received contents to these two waybills?” is more actionable than “My parcel is wrong.” Include enough context to locate the box without sharing private address details in a public forum.

## Close the inbound record before arranging overseas delivery
Once the expected units are accounted for, record their warehouse references beside the seller order. Keep any unresolved discrepancy visible before selecting items for international shipment. If an item needs a photo, an identifying label read or an answer from the merchant, complete that check while the warehouse can still inspect it. Only the confirmed goods should enter your outbound packing plan.

Your final record should let you answer a simple question without reopening five websites: what arrived, under which domestic waybill, for which approved request, and what will happen to it next? Keep the outgoing parcel number in a new field when one exists. The forwarding request, incoming carton and international parcel represent different stages. Preserving the links between them makes later tracking and customer-service questions considerably easier.

If the seller ships a replacement later, treat the new waybill as another incoming shipment in your records and ask what the current forwarding procedure requires. Labelling the new entry as a replacement explains its relationship to the purchase without falsely implying that both units arrived in the first box.
