# How to match a domestic tracking number to a Superbuy warehouse entry
A delivery notification from a seller can be accurate while your Superbuy warehouse list still looks incomplete. The two records are answering different questions. One reports what a carrier did with a parcel. The other records what was received, identified and stocked for your account. Before deciding which party to contact, follow the particular box across that boundary.

## Draw the short journey first
Start with the journey inside the country where the seller dispatches the goods. Your marketplace order links a buyer and a merchant. The merchant hands a box to a domestic carrier, which transports it to a receiving address. The receiving warehouse then identifies, inspects and records the goods. International shipment is a later choice. A tracking update mentioning delivery on the domestic leg cannot establish that an overseas parcel is on its way.

For an agent purchase, find the Superbuy shopping order associated with the original item. For a purchase you made yourself, find the forwarding request and the logistics information you supplied. Superbuy's forwarding guide directs the user to provide the inbound shipping details after the item is reviewed. Check which of those two starting points describes your case. A shopping order number from a similar purchase can lead you to inspect the wrong record.

## Copy the carrier evidence exactly
Open the seller's dispatch detail and identify the carrier and waybill together. Copy the full waybill as text, preserving any leading zeroes. Save the most recent event, its date and its stated location. A screenshot cropped down to the word “delivered” omits the information needed to connect the event to a particular carton. If a platform shows a tracking alias, keep the original carrier number where available.

Suppose two purchases appear in your marketplace history with almost identical titles and consecutive order dates. On one, the waybill ends in 381; on the other, it ends in 318. A glance at the first digits may suggest that both refer to the delivery you just received. Compare the entire string. When you later write to support, paste the complete number rather than the last three digits used in your private notes.

## Identify what the last event actually says
Read the wording of the event as well as the status colour. Label created, collected, out for delivery, delivered and returned to sender indicate different stages. A label can exist before the merchant hands over the box. An out-for-delivery update describes work in progress. A delivery event names an endpoint for the carrier's movement; it does not list the contents counted by warehouse staff.

Look at the destination alongside the event. Does the city and receiving address correspond to the warehouse selected on your request? An account login or internal recipient identifier can help connect the parcel with the customer after delivery. If the marketplace receipt shows a different address from the one in Superbuy, keep both records and explain the difference. Do not “correct” the screenshot before sharing it with official support: the original discrepancy may be exactly what identifies the problem.

## Look for an intake match in My Warehouse
Open My Warehouse for the relevant account. Superbuy's official guidance identifies this as the place to view stocked items. Review the warehouse entries around the delivery event. Check the item description, purchase or forwarding reference, quantity and available pictures. A translated title may differ substantially from the seller's original wording; use identifiers wherever possible.

An inbound carrier parcel and a warehouse inventory entry need not have the same shape in your notes. One carton might hold several items. One seller purchase might produce two cartons. If the warehouse lists separate goods while your evidence describes a single carrier box, group those entries under the waybill rather than deleting one as a duplicate. Conversely, two received cartons cannot be reconciled solely from one product title without determining what each contained.

## Work through a split-delivery example
Imagine buying a desk organiser and a pen tray from one seller. The retailer issues two domestic waybills. The organiser shows a delivery scan, while the tray is still moving. You see one warehouse entry described as a desk accessory. Before reporting a missing piece, compare the first tracking number with the warehouse reference and inspect the photo. If it depicts the organiser, the current records are consistent: one item is stocked and the other is still in transit.

Now change one detail. The merchant says both items were in the first carton and the second number was a cancelled replacement label. The warehouse picture still shows only the organiser. The open question has changed. It now concerns the contents of a delivered carton and the status of a second unused label. Ask the merchant to confirm its packing list. Ask Superbuy which items were recorded under the first waybill. Keep the distinction clear and avoid asking a carrier to locate an item that may never have travelled under its number.

## Route the inquiry to the party that can inspect the evidence
If the carrier shows only creation of a label, the merchant can usually clarify whether it actually dispatched that carton. If the event names the wrong destination, ask the sender and carrier about the address they used. If the correct parcel shows delivery to the intended warehouse but you cannot match it with an entry, Superbuy can investigate its receiving records. Explain which of these checks you have already completed.

Use a concise opening sentence: “I am trying to match a domestic delivery to my forwarding record.” Follow with the forwarding or purchase reference, carrier, complete waybill, date and location of the delivery event, and expected product and quantity. State exactly what My Warehouse currently shows. A reply that confirms receipt of the box may still leave a separate question about contents; acknowledge the first point before asking about the second.

## Avoid multiplying records while a check is open
It is tempting to enter the parcel again with a slightly different product title, especially if searching your account yields no result. First ask whether the existing forwarding or shopping record can be updated. Duplicate submissions can make it harder to tell which record describes the actual box. A new title cannot change which address is on the label or which carrier made the delivery.

Keep a short activity note with dates and references rather than sending disconnected messages. If support requests a clearer proof of delivery, append it to the same inquiry when possible. If the seller supplies a corrected waybill, label the old one as superseded. This preserves what was known at the time of the first search while directing the next search to the better evidence.

## Know when the domestic question is settled
Mark a domestic shipment as matched when you can connect the carrier record, the correct purchase or forwarding request and the relevant warehouse entry or entries. Mark the contents separately if one piece remains unidentified. That division keeps the status accurate: “carton received, tray unconfirmed” tells you much more than simply “delivered.”

Later, if you submit the stocked items for international delivery, save the outbound parcel number in its own place. Look under the parcel record for the international journey. Keep the domestic references attached to the purchases they originally described. If another piece turns up or a seller answers a question much later, you can add that evidence without confusing it with a different shipment heading to your home. If the final warehouse count differs from the seller invoice, keep the count discrepancy open even after matching the waybill. Finding the carton and confirming its contents are separate outcomes.
