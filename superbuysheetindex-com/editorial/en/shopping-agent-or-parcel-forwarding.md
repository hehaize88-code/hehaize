# Superbuy shopping agent or parcel forwarding: which order should you create?
A shop link in a spreadsheet tells you where an item is listed. It does not tell you who will buy it. On Superbuy, the answer to that question determines whether you need a shopping order or a forwarding request. Choose before money changes hands: the same pair of shoes can travel through the same warehouse under either arrangement, but the person responsible for the purchase and the record that identifies the shipment are different.

## Follow the payment to the seller
Start by asking who is going to pay the marketplace seller. If you have picked a listing and want Superbuy to buy it for you, use the shopping agent process. Superbuy's app guide describes choosing the product options and quantity, paying for the purchase and any applicable domestic delivery, and then letting the agent contact the seller. Your first useful reference is the shopping order.

If you have already bought the shoes from the seller, you have a purchase record somewhere else. Paying for another shopping order in Superbuy could commission a second pair. The forwarding process is designed to register goods that someone else will send to the warehouse. Locate the seller's confirmation, check the receiving instructions and register the parcel under the applicable forwarding procedure. If the seller has already dispatched it, tell support the true status when asking what can still be matched.

## A seller link is useful in both workflows
A product link can identify the intended purchase in a shopping order. It can also help describe the contents of a forwarding request. Its presence on a form does not turn forwarding into a purchase. Look at what the service will do next. Will someone contact the merchant and buy the item? Or are you supplying information for a parcel that you or your seller will send?

Consider an illustrative case. You find a small stationery set on a marketplace and save its link. Nobody has paid. You want the agent to select the navy cover and purchase one set. Your instructions should identify the cover, quantity, exact seller option and anything you need clarified. Contrast a second case: the seller has already accepted your payment for a navy set and asks for a local delivery address. You now need the correct warehouse destination and a way to identify the resulting package. A new instruction to purchase stationery does not solve that problem.

## Know who can answer the next question
For an agent purchase, communicate order questions through the agent order. If the retailer changes the available colour after you pay, ask about that order before accepting a substitution. Keep the original option text and confirm whether the proposed replacement still meets your needs. The relevant question at this stage concerns the purchase from the merchant.

With forwarding, the seller relationship may be your own. If the merchant has not dispatched the goods, ask that merchant for dispatch information. If a carrier has delivered a package to the Superbuy address but you cannot find its warehouse entry, gather the waybill and forwarding reference for the warehouse. Sending a refund question to a warehouse that did not buy the item can waste time while the seller's return conditions continue to apply.

## Make a budget for the route you actually use
Write down what has already been paid before comparing prices. For an agent purchase, separate the item subtotal and domestic delivery from the eventual overseas parcel. For a self-purchase, the marketplace receipt already represents the goods. Keep that receipt as an item cost even if the forwarding form asks you to record the item's value. Recording its value does not mean you should count a second purchase in your budget.

Imagine a seller invoice of 150 in the seller's currency, plus 10 for domestic delivery. If you paid those amounts directly, your running purchase cost is 160. Entering 150 as an item value on a forwarding worksheet does not raise the running cost to 310. Add actual new charges when they occur, identifying the service and currency for each. These figures illustrate bookkeeping only; the applicable amounts come from your own order.

## The receiving address belongs to a specific request
Superbuy's forwarding guide explains that an account-specific identifier helps distinguish incoming parcels. Read the full warehouse address shown in your own account. Compare it to the selected warehouse and to the address the seller has accepted. Pay attention to the final part of the receiving details when copying them into a marketplace form with separate fields.

If a seller truncates a field, compare the saved order address after checkout. Ask the seller whether the complete receiving information will appear on the physical label. If something is missing, keep the carrier reference and tell Superbuy which address the seller actually used. An edited address in your profile does not retroactively alter a label already attached to a parcel.

## Keep one record per purchase and one per shipment
A useful shopping note contains a product reference, option, quantity, amount paid and merchant. A forwarding note needs the receiving warehouse, forwarding request, carrier and actual waybill. One purchase can produce several waybills. Several purchases can travel together in one box. Add a link between records instead of treating the shopping number, inbound waybill and outbound tracking number as interchangeable.

For example, a seller may send a notebook today and its separately ordered refill tomorrow. The seller order can be the same on both dispatch notices while the carrier numbers differ. To check whether both pieces arrived, match each waybill and then compare the contents of the warehouse entries. An account search for the ordinary-language title “notebook” alone may miss a refill entered under a different name.

## Where the two workflows meet
After intake, the warehouse stage has more in common. The official Superbuy guides direct customers to My Warehouse to review stocked items before choosing what to ship. At that point your decision concerns the physical units in the warehouse. Confirm the relevant options and quantities, resolve any problem you need to address locally, and select the correct units for the international parcel.

Keep the original relationship visible even after consolidation. If two shirts came through agent purchases and a bag came from a shop you paid directly, one outbound parcel may eventually contain all three. A problem with international delivery can concern the entire parcel. A wrong colour on the bag may still lead you back to your direct seller order. A simple list stating who bought each item prevents the combined parcel from erasing that distinction.

## Decide what to do when the status is unclear
If you cannot remember whether an order was completed, check the seller account, your payment record and Superbuy's order list before submitting a new request. A saved product or cart entry is not evidence that the seller was paid. A posted payment may also need investigation if its order reference cannot be identified. Record what each system actually shows and ask a specific question about the unmatched record.

Before proceeding, complete this sentence in your own notes: “The seller will be paid by ___, the parcel will be sent by ___, and I will identify it to Superbuy with ___.” If the first blank is the agent, start from your shopping order. If you already paid the merchant, consult the forwarding guide and its review procedure. If any blank is still uncertain, resolve that one point first. It is much easier to clarify a missing reference before dispatch than to distinguish two identical purchases after both arrive. Keep the answer with the original receipt or order confirmation.
