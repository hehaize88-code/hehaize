# A self-purchased parcel is missing from Superbuy: prepare an intake investigation
You have a forwarding request, your seller says the package arrived and My Warehouse does not show the expected item. The most useful first step is to build a short investigation with one clearly defined missing link. Establish what the seller dispatched, where the carrier took it and what your Superbuy account shows. Each party can then check the part of the journey it actually handled.

## State the problem without deciding the cause
Write a sentence containing an observation and a question. “The domestic carrier records delivery of waybill X to the selected warehouse on Tuesday; I cannot match it to a stocked entry. Can you help identify it?” is specific. It does not require you to decide prematurely whether the merchant, carrier, warehouse or account entry is at fault.

Avoid treating a missing entry as proof that the parcel was lost. The available evidence might instead describe an unused label, a delivery to a different warehouse, an unrecognized sender, an incomplete account identifier or a product recorded under an unexpected name. List only possibilities supported by something in your own records. If the seller has never supplied a collected waybill, the first unresolved step is dispatch, not warehouse intake.

## Build a timeline from original records
Create one row per event with the time, source and exact reference. Useful events include the marketplace purchase, forwarding request, review response, seller dispatch notice, carrier collection, last carrier scan and any warehouse message. Preserve the timezone shown by each source when it matters. Two events labelled “Tuesday” can occur on different local dates in the buyer's country and at the warehouse.

Order the events by what occurred rather than the sequence in which notifications appeared on your phone. A delayed email can arrive after a newer carrier update. An order page may show an edited tracking number beneath an older dispatch date. If a time is unknown, leave it unknown. A timeline built from explicit events is more valuable than a polished story that invents the missing transition.

## Check the receiving address and account identifier
Open your own Superbuy forwarding instructions and compare them with the address on the seller's confirmed order. Match warehouse, region, street and the account-specific information used to identify incoming shipments. Superbuy's guide explains the purpose of the identification number appearing with a receiving address. A seller may have copied the street correctly while shortening the last field on the printed label.

If you find a difference, capture both records privately and describe it precisely. “The seller receipt shows the correct street but is missing the final identifier” gives the receiving team a concrete search clue. Do not substitute the corrected address for the original evidence. Likewise, do not rely on a profile update to correct a package already in transit. Ask the seller or carrier what address appears on the actual shipment when the order receipt is inconclusive.

## Test the tracking number before sending it
Check that the number belongs to the courier that physically handled the parcel. Distinguish it from a seller order, forwarding application, marketplace consolidation number or an international tracking number from a previous shipment. Open the merchant's shipping record and compare the carrier and complete number with what you entered in Superbuy. Leading zeroes, letters and transposed digits can all affect a match.

A merchant may issue a label and then reprint a replacement. Ask which number has an actual collection event. If the replacement is the one that moved, name the original as an unused or superseded reference in the inquiry. That tells support why two numbers appear in the paperwork. If neither number has a collection event, ask the seller for evidence of handover before asking the warehouse to search for a delivered box.

## Specify the box and its expected contents
For a single parcel, list the useful physical identifiers: type of item, selected option, quantity, seller name and any packing information provided. For a split delivery, provide a waybill-to-contents map. Avoid assuming that everything on one seller order was packed together. The merchant can often confirm which pieces accompanied a particular label.

Imagine two cartons listed as a bookcase frame and a shelf pack. The frame appears in My Warehouse, but the shelf pack does not. The support inquiry should name the unmatched shelf pack, its own waybill and its expected pieces. Sending only the original order title “bookcase” could lead to a reply identifying the already matched frame. Including the known warehouse entry is useful because it shows exactly which part of the purchase has been accounted for.

## Compare intake evidence before choosing the recipient
If the last evidence is a marketplace status saying the seller prepared the order, the seller can clarify actual dispatch. If a carrier records an attempted delivery or an address problem, ask about the unresolved delivery event. If the carrier confirms delivery to the expected destination and the logistics details were recorded, use the Superbuy support route associated with the forwarding request. Each inquiry should ask for an action the recipient can perform.

For a warehouse inquiry, include the request reference, selected warehouse, domestic carrier and waybill, delivery event, expected contents and any existing warehouse record that could be related. Explain which account view you checked. Share full identifying details through the official private channel. Crop unrelated orders and payment information from supporting screenshots, while keeping the specific reference, date and event legible.

## Ask one searchable question at a time
A first message might read: “Please check whether the carton under this domestic waybill was matched to my forwarding request. The carrier shows delivery to the selected warehouse. I attach the seller dispatch confirmation and the delivery scan. The expected contents are one shelf pack; the frame from the same seller is already stocked under a separate entry.” Add the real references in the appropriate private fields.

If the response confirms receipt but says the contents were different, the investigation has moved forward. Ask which item was recorded from that waybill and compare it with the seller's packing statement. If the warehouse needs a photo of the physical label, request it from the seller rather than repeatedly resending a screenshot of the product itself. Preserve the question and response together so the next step builds on what has already been established.

## Close the case only after the missing link is identified
When an entry appears, compare its physical description and quantity with what was expected. An entry with the same translated product title may belong to an earlier order. Confirm the connection using the forwarding reference, carrier evidence or the warehouse's explicit identification. Record what resolved the discrepancy and retain any outstanding quality or quantity question separately.

A delivery investigation can finish with a matched item, a correction to the sender's record, a carrier inquiry or another documented outcome. Record the actual outcome rather than forcing every case into “delivered.” If you subsequently send the goods overseas, create a new outbound parcel entry. The inbound investigation then remains a complete account of how the warehouse identified the package, ready to consult if the seller or carrier asks about it later.

If you ultimately discover that the parcel was stocked under an unexpected description, note the phrase used by the warehouse and the evidence that established the match. It can be useful to record a picture reference and the original seller description next to that entry. The warehouse title may stay unchanged even after support confirms its identity. If it is later split across parcels, retain the original reference beside both outbound records so you can identify the right portion at delivery.
