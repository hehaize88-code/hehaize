# Using Superbuy on a phone: check the option that reaches your shopping order
A mobile screen makes it easy to save a photograph and hard to see the complete purchase in one view. The image, selected variant, quantity and price can appear on separate panels. Superbuy's app guide describes finding a product, selecting its options and asking the agent to purchase it. Use those stages as checkpoints. The aim is to carry your intended selection all the way from the seller listing to the paid order.

## Begin with a recoverable product reference
Before switching apps, save the original marketplace product link. If a share sheet includes promotional text around the address, preserve the underlying listing address where possible. Make a short note of the seller and item identifier. That gives you a way back if opening a shopping link changes the active browser tab or if a marketplace app returns to its home screen.

A screenshot is a useful companion, especially for a distinctive pattern. It rarely contains all the information required to identify a live listing. The same studio picture can appear on multiple products or be reused by different sellers. A saved image showing a blue notebook does not establish which shop offers it, what paper is inside or whether the photograph depicts the cover only. Save the link first and the picture second.

## Check the handoff to Superbuy
The official app guide describes searching from the home screen and recognizing certain product links. Use the available entry method on your device. When the agent page opens, compare its product and seller information with what you intended to import. Do not infer a match merely because the first image looks familiar.

If the device opens a search result list rather than the exact product, treat this as a new identification step. Compare the original item number where available, then examine the option set and seller details. A search result with another seller and a lower displayed price is a separate purchasing choice. If you still want it, review it as a new listing rather than silently replacing the earlier selection in your notes.

## Read the entire option panel
Open the selector and identify the dimensions of the choice. A product can have colour, size, bundle contents and quantity, each controlled separately. Some combinations may be unavailable even if the colour swatch itself appears selectable. Make the complete selection, then look for the final summary. Scroll horizontally or expand text where the interface abbreviates a long option label.

Imagine a listing for sketchbooks with “black A5, dotted” and “black A5, blank.” The thumbnail can be identical because it shows the closed cover. If your requirement is blank paper, the word at the end is the decisive feature. Save the complete option text. A hand-written note saying “black notebook” loses precisely the detail that the warehouse may later need to verify.

## Check the unit and the number you are ordering
A quantity selector counts the unit represented by the chosen option. If the option says a pack of three, setting the quantity to two can mean six physical pieces. If the price refers to a replacement cover, it may omit the book displayed in a lifestyle photograph. Read the unit description and the subtotal after making the selection.

An illustrative check is to write “two packs, three pieces per pack, six pieces expected” before checkout. Compare that reading with the displayed quantity and the merchant's option description. If one screen describes two units and another describes six pieces, they may be consistent. If you cannot explain the difference yourself, ask the agent to clarify before authorizing a purchase. Do not try to fix an unknown unit by choosing a larger number and hoping the seller interprets it correctly.

## Use the cart as a final specification
After adding the item, reopen its cart line. Check the exact option, quantity, seller and current price. A previously saved cart item can survive between sessions and look like a second copy of today's item. Compare dates or line details before changing quantities. Replacing an old option with a new line can leave both selected for payment.

Read each charge in context. Superbuy's shopping guide separates the purchase and any applicable domestic delivery from the later international parcel process. Save the currency and total on the purchase screen. If a catalogue converts a price for convenience, use that figure for rough comparison and use the actual checkout amount for your payment record. A mobile notification quoting a card amount may use a different currency and cannot by itself identify the selected item.

## Write notes that resolve a decision
An order note works best when it identifies an option the agent can check. Instead of “as pictured,” write the original seller option followed by your explanation of what matters. For a sketchbook, that might be “black cover, A5, blank pages; please ask before replacing with dotted paper.” If the seller allows customization, reproduce spelling and case carefully and specify any uncertainties before payment.

Avoid using a note to contradict a selected option without checking whether the seller can supply the request. Selecting the cheaper cover and writing “complete book please” does not establish that the full set has been purchased. If a required combination is absent from the live selector, ask whether it is available and what it costs. Save the response with the order confirmation.

## Follow the shopping order into the warehouse
Once payment is complete, save the Superbuy order reference. Use the order inquiry or other contact route shown for questions about that purchase. A question about an unavailable colour belongs with the shopping record; a shipping quote for an assembled outbound parcel belongs later in the process. Naming the stage helps the person responding understand what action you expect.

When the item is stocked, the official guide points you to My Warehouse. Reopen your note alongside the warehouse pictures. Check what is visible: a closed black cover might match the colour but not the page ruling. If the interior matters, request a view that shows the relevant feature and check the available inspection options for that item. Keep the item reference in the request so a photo of a similar notebook cannot be accidentally used to make your decision.

## Recover calmly if an app switch loses your place
If the app closes during checkout or payment, inspect the order and payment records before repeating the action. A checkout screen reopening at its beginning does not establish whether the payment succeeded. Look for the order reference, the amount, currency and latest payment state. If they disagree, use the official support channel with those details. Avoid creating a second order merely to see whether the first one existed.

Finish by keeping a compact mobile record: seller link, complete option, unit and quantity, paid order reference and the feature that must be checked at intake. Once you have those fields, changing devices or reopening a cart becomes manageable. You can explain exactly what you commissioned, recognize your own warehouse item and handle freight as a new decision when the item is ready. That continuity is more valuable than any particular arrangement of buttons in an app update.

For purchases made for someone else, send the final selected option back to that person before checkout if the choice was ambiguous. Ask them to confirm the exact attribute at issue, such as ruling, sleeve length or included accessory. A reply approving a clear written specification is easier to use later than a thumbs-up attached to a general product photo. Save the final confirmation alongside the original order record.
