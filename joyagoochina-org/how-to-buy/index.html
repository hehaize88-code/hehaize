How to buy with Joyagoo

Understand product selection, the first payment, warehouse QC and international parcel submission.

01

Find the right listing and build a clear order

A Joyagoo spreadsheet is useful because it turns a large catalogue into a manageable starting point. It can group shoes, clothing, accessories and electronics and send you toward a specific product. It cannot freeze a seller’s price, stock, colour choices or size options. Those details live on the current product page and may change after the spreadsheet entry was added.

Open the matching product page before making any decision. Compare the image, title, available variants, seller notes and domestic delivery charge with the item you intended to find. Save the original marketplace link as well. Joyagoo’s published shopping guidance says users can search with links from Taobao, Tmall, JD, 1688, Yupoo and Weidian, while some harder-to-search sources may require a DIY order.

This distinction matters for both shopping and SEO: a useful product directory helps a reader find the right page, while a trustworthy guide explains what must still be checked. Treat every product card as a route into fresh information rather than evidence that an old screenshot is still accurate.

Once the correct listing is open, choose the variant carefully. A colour name such as black may cover several versions, and a size label does not prove the real garment or insole measurement. Read seller notes, inspect the variant thumbnail and use the order remark field only for details the listing does not already capture. Vague remarks create more room for a purchasing mistake.

For a DIY order, the burden is higher because the agent may not be able to read a normal product page automatically. Supply a working source link, a clear product name, the selected specification, quantity, expected product price and any necessary contact detail from the seller. Do not use a screenshot alone when a stable source link is available; screenshots hide stock changes and may omit the selected option.

Before payment, review the cart line by line. Two similar products from the same spreadsheet can have different sellers, delivery charges or return conditions. If a product is time-sensitive, ask the agent a specific question before the purchase rather than assuming a general platform guide overrides the seller’s rules.

First practical check

Confirm the exact colour, size and quantity on the live product page before adding the item to an agent cart.

02

Understand payments and order statuses

Joyagoo’s fee explanation states that its shopping-agent service is free. That statement does not mean the whole order has no additional cost. The first payment covers the goods and the Chinese domestic shipping fee charged for moving the order from the seller to the warehouse. The published explanation says that money is transferred to the seller through the platform.

This is the first of two main payment stages. International delivery is not normally included at this point because the packed parcel does not yet exist. Optional services, return shipping, payment-related charges and later international freight can still add cost. A cheap product price should therefore be read as the warehouse-bound cost, not the final delivered price.

Keep enough flexibility in the budget for the second payment. Bulky packaging, a destination with limited routes or a product with mailing restrictions can make delivery more expensive than the item itself. The most useful comparison is not simply product A versus product B; it is the likely packed parcel and available route for each choice.

Joyagoo’s published workflow describes a sequence from Order Processing to Purchased, Seller Sent, Stock Arrived, QC Completed and Stored in Warehouse. Order Processing means the agent is accepting and preparing the purchase. Purchased means the buying step has been completed, but it does not mean the seller has already dispatched the item.

Seller Sent marks the seller-to-warehouse movement. The official guide warns that a seller may take time to dispatch and that the agent does not control the seller’s speed. A purchased order that has not moved for several days is therefore not automatically lost; it may be waiting for seller action. Use the order contact function for a focused status question.

Stock Arrived and QC Completed are more important decision points than many new buyers realize. Warehouse arrival starts the inspection stage, and the QC result gives you evidence to compare with the listing. Do not wait for every order in a large haul before checking the first item’s photos, because the return clock is tied to the individual QC completion.

03

Review QC and protect the return window

The published buying guide says warehouse staff carry out a quality check and take several photos after the product arrives. Those images are not a promise of authenticity or perfect construction. They are evidence that lets you compare the received item with the selected seller variant before deciding whether to store, return or later ship it.

Start with the overall silhouette and colour, then move to stitching, print placement, hardware, sole bonding, labels and packaging. For clothing and footwear, a tag photo is not the same as a physical measurement. When fit depends on length or width, request a precise ruler photo instead of asking for a general closer picture.

Joyagoo’s value-added-services table lists an extra detailed photo at $0.15 each, whether requested during the product order or after warehouse arrival. Use that service only with a clear instruction: identify the surface, angle or measurement you need. A single purposeful photo can be more useful than several broad pictures.

Joyagoo’s return policy gives a five-day, or 120-hour, request window after an order changes to QC Completed. This is a request window rather than an unconditional promise that every item will be accepted. Seller participation, product category, condition and packaging requirements still determine whether the return can proceed.

The policy lists several possible exclusions, including customised goods, some opened products, items missing tags or accessories, underwear, food, second-hand goods and other seller-defined categories. It also says an eligible item should remain new and resalable. Keep original tags, boxes, accessories and protective materials until you finish the QC decision.

If the return control is no longer visible in the order area, the published policy says the returnable period may already have passed. A spreadsheet listing does not change that clock. Inspect each new QC set promptly, describe any problem with reference to a specific image and submit the request while the option is available.

04

Use storage and plan the packed parcel

Joyagoo’s shopping guidance states that each product receives 90 days of free warehouse storage. That period can be useful when purchases arrive at different times and you want to combine them into one international parcel. It is much longer than the return window, so the two clocks must never be confused.

The value-added-services table says a paid storage extension can apply to products stored between 60 and 90 days, with a published maximum storage time of six months and a listed fee of $1.50 per product ID per month. Check the current account display before relying on an extension, because service prices and eligibility can change.

A practical warehouse plan records the arrival date, QC deadline, expected remaining purchases and target parcel date. Consolidation can reduce duplicated packaging, but an oversized mixed parcel may trigger volumetric billing or fewer route choices. Combine products because the parcel makes sense, not simply because free days remain.

When the selected items are stored, the user submits a parcel, enters the destination, chooses from the routes available for those products and pays international shipping. The official guide explains that ordinary parcel submission starts with an estimated fee. After packing, the warehouse calculates the actual shipping amount and the balance may need to be adjusted.

Rehearsal packing reverses the order of that decision by packing and measuring before the final international payment. Joyagoo’s shopping guide describes the service as 20 CNY, while the value-added-services table lists $3 per parcel. Because both figures appear in current published material, this guide does not pretend they are identical; use the price shown in the account when ordering.

Rehearsal can be useful for shoe boxes, winter coats, protective packaging and mixed parcels whose size is difficult to predict. It is not automatically necessary for every small parcel. Decide whether the measurement uncertainty is large enough to justify the service.

Published-price mismatch

Joyagoo currently publishes both 20 CNY and $3 for rehearsal packing on different help pages. Treat the account checkout price as current.

05

Choose shipping services and track delivery

The route list is filtered by destination and by the products in the parcel. Mailing restrictions can remove a line that another buyer used successfully, so a review from a different country is not a quotation for your order. Compare the available routes at parcel submission and read their restrictions, billing basis and insurance terms.

The official workflow mentions services such as packaging removal, reinforcement and insurance. The value-added table also lists parcel photography at $0.75 per parcel, with one image before sealing and one after sealing. Removing a box may reduce volume, but it may also remove protection; reinforcement may add weight but reduce damage risk.

Coupons are applied to international shipping in the published workflow and cannot be stacked. A coupon does not reduce the seller’s product price. Check its expiry, minimum spend and eligible routes before designing a parcel around the advertised value.

After dispatch, use the tracking details in the parcel area. A tracking number may exist before the first carrier scan, and weekends or public holidays can delay pickup. Joyagoo’s shipping page states that provider schedules differ and that logistics companies may not operate on non-working days.

Published delivery ranges are broad: air freight is described as roughly 10–20 days, ocean freight as 45–85 days and rail freight as 40–60 days after leaving the warehouse. These are general ranges rather than promises for every country, line or customs event. Actual delivery can be affected by handoffs, inspections, taxes, weather and peak-season volume.

The safest buying process is therefore a chain of checks: verify the live listing, understand the first payment, watch the order status, review QC within the deadline, plan the parcel using its packed characteristics and track the selected line. A good spreadsheet gets you to the correct product; careful decisions after that are what make it useful.
