Joyagoo Order Status Explained: Stock Arrived, QC Completed and Stored

Understand Joyagoo order statuses, check what Stock Arrived means, distinguish QC from storage, and identify the next action before shipping.

01

Start with the exact order and its latest event

Stock Arrived means the item has reached the warehouse stage and is awaiting quality checking and storage, according to Joyagoo's order-status guidance. It does not mean your international parcel has departed. QC Completed and Stored in Warehouse are later labels with different purposes. Begin with the individual order record, then check the photographs, available controls and messages attached to that same item. A shipment containing several products can have different progress for every order.

The practical difficulty is that buyers often use one word, shipping, for several separate movements. A seller can send an item to the warehouse while your international parcel does not yet exist. Similarly, an order can be ready for inspection while another purchase is still waiting for dispatch. Write down the order identifier, selected variation, latest status, event date and any action requested. This small record is more reliable than remembering a coloured badge or comparing your progress with another buyer's screenshot.

02

Separate purchase progress from a request for your input

The first useful distinction is between work happening in the background and an instruction that needs your response. The official status list uses Purchased for a completed purchase awaiting delivery and Seller Sent for seller dispatch. Clear Balance indicates an additional amount, while Confirmation Pending calls for confirmation. Read the associated message before doing anything: the label alone does not tell you which specification, cost or discrepancy needs attention.

If a message concerns a size or colour, compare it with your paid variation and the original listing. If it concerns money, request or inspect the explanation and distinguish an adjustment from a second copy of an existing payment. Keep the transaction record beside the order. Do not create a replacement order merely because the first appears slow; that can produce two purchases and two sets of costs. Contact the agent through the existing order so the answer stays attached to the relevant item and earlier discussion.

03

Use Stock Arrived as an inspection handoff

An arrival label is the point to prepare your inspection criteria. Decide which visible features would change your acceptance decision before the photos appear. For shoes this might include the selected size label, a side view, matching left and right pairs, or an insole measurement. For clothing it could include chest width, garment length, the back design and fasteners. Keep the list short enough for a specific photograph or measurement to settle each uncertainty.

If the seller's domestic tracking and the order screen appear to disagree, record both timestamps and the destination shown by the carrier. Ask the agent to reconcile the domestic shipment with the order rather than assuming a delivered scan proves that every warehouse task is complete. A carrier event, warehouse receipt and published QC images are different pieces of evidence. Avoid repeatedly changing packaging instructions while identification is unresolved; original labels and packaging can help the warehouse locate and verify the item.

04

Review QC before using storage as a waiting room

Once photographs are available, inspect the paid item rather than a representative product image. Compare quantity, variation, visible damage and any measurements that matter to you. A completed warehouse check does not prove that a garment fits your body or that a photograph establishes authenticity. Separate what the images actually show from what remains uncertain. If a missing angle prevents a decision, make a targeted request and keep the request date in your order record.

Do not assume that requesting another photograph stops an after-sales deadline. Check the current return control and its conditions while waiting for evidence. Preserve labels, accessories and retail packaging until you have decided whether to keep the item. Asking for box removal first can conflict with a later request to return it. The dedicated return and storage guide explains the timing rules; here the operational rule is to finish the inspection decision before treating the item as something that can simply wait for the rest of your haul.

05

Move from individual orders to one parcel plan

After deciding which items to keep, make a parcel list using the accepted orders. For each one, record restrictions, dimensions if known, packaging needs and the remaining storage period shown in the account. An item being stored does not mean every delivery route will accept it. Review the lines offered for your actual selection and destination. Keep fragile products, batteries or other restricted contents visible in your planning record instead of hiding them inside a general description such as accessories.

A hypothetical example shows why this matters. Suppose a jacket and a pair of shoes are accepted, but a third item needs a replacement photograph. You can compare waiting for all three with submitting the two accepted items separately. The useful comparison includes repeated base charges, packaging space and the oldest storage deadline. It is not enough to assume that one larger parcel is automatically better. Keep the unresolved item out of an irreversible packing decision until you have the information needed to accept or return it.

06

Read parcel payment and dispatch as a second workflow

The parcel record has its own messages, separate from product purchasing. Check whether the screen is asking you to review a quote, pay a charge, confirm a route or wait for processing. Confirm the selected orders and destination before payment. A saved quote should show the measurement stage and currency, because an estimate prepared before packing may differ from a later measured amount. Save the payment confirmation with the parcel identifier rather than only with one product order.

Treat an international dispatch event as the beginning of the transport record, not the end of your checks. Note the route name, tracking reference and the date that the handoff was recorded. These details let you investigate a later tracking gap without confusing it with the earlier domestic delivery. If the account contains a cancellation or replacement parcel, make sure you are following the current identifier. Old parcel numbers can remain in screenshots and messages long after the active plan has changed.

07

Track a return without confusing it with parcel dispatch

A return creates a different direction of travel. Keep the product order and the return discussion together, and read each update as part of the seller remedy rather than part of your international shipment. If the agent requests a fee or approval, compare that request with the agreed remedy. Keep the amount, reason and confirmation in the same record. Do not assume that an item moving back to a seller is already a completed refund.

When a remedy is recorded as complete, reconcile it with the account transaction history. Check which item price was refunded, whether deductions were documented and where the amount was credited. An account balance and an external payment account are different destinations. If you later make a replacement purchase, give it a separate row so its inspection and storage dates do not inherit those of the returned item. This prevents one cancelled purchase from making the rest of the haul difficult to follow.

08

Ask for the missing fact when progress is unclear

A useful support request identifies one unresolved transition. State the item or parcel identifier, the last visible event and date, what you already checked, and what you need confirmed. For example, you might ask whether the domestic shipment has been matched to the order, whether a requested measurement is pending, or whether a paid parcel has a confirmed carrier handoff. Keep private identifiers inside the account conversation; a public post does not need your full label, address or tracking number.

Compare the response with your record and update the next action. A request for your confirmation requires a decision; an investigation may require a documented follow-up date. Neither should be replaced with repeated payments or duplicate orders. Keep product acceptance, storage planning, parcel payment and transport tracking as separate steps. This makes even a mixed haul understandable: each item has a current decision, each parcel has a current owner of the next action, and each important claim has a dated piece of evidence behind it.
