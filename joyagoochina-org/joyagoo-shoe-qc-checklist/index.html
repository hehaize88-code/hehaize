Joyagoo Shoe QC Checklist: Sizes, Pairs, Soles and Photos

Inspect Joyagoo shoe QC photos for matching pairs, size labels, visible damage and missing angles, then decide what to clarify before shipping.

01

Match the photographs to the shoes you actually ordered

A useful Joyagoo shoe QC review starts with order identity, not a judgement about how attractive the pair looks. Open the paid order and compare the selected model, colour, size and quantity with the warehouse photographs. Check that the images show both shoes from that order. Seller studio photographs and images from another buyer can help you understand a design, but they do not show the condition of the pair currently waiting for your acceptance decision.

Keep the product link, variation and order identifier beside the photos. If a detail seems inconsistent, describe the difference in those terms rather than saying only that the shoes look wrong. A box label is helpful, but it should not replace the labels or markings on the shoes themselves. Packaging can be mismatched. Check the item first, then the packaging and accessories, and record any missing evidence before deciding whether the pair is ready to join your international parcel.

02

Check size labels on both shoes and understand their limits

Look for a readable size label on each shoe and compare them with one another and with the ordered size system. A correct label confirms part of the specification; it does not prove that the shoe will fit. Different shapes, widths, linings and toe boxes can make shoes with the same nominal size feel different. Use the dedicated size guide to compare the seller's chart with a suitable reference pair rather than converting every label through one generic size table.

If an internal measurement is important, ask whether the warehouse can provide it safely and what method they will use. An insole measurement, an outsole measurement and your foot length are not interchangeable. Request a photograph with the start and end of the tape visible and avoid instructions that require damaging a fixed insole. If the measurement remains uncertain, record that uncertainty explicitly. A long-looking outsole in a photograph should not be used to overrule a conflicting size label or a clearly unsuitable seller chart.

03

Compare the pair from matching angles

Ask for views that let you compare the left and right shoe under similar conditions. A top view can reveal differences in lacing, tongue position or overall shape. Front and rear views help you compare toe areas and heel alignment. Side views show panels and sole joins, while an outsole view shows the underside. You do not need every conceivable photograph; choose the angles that would actually resolve a concern about your pair or its ordered specification.

Perspective matters. A shoe closer to the camera can look larger, and one tilted heel can appear uneven even when the pair is aligned normally. Before calling an apparent difference a defect, ask for a level comparison on the same surface if that would settle the question. Keep your request specific about the angle and feature. A repeated wide shot from the same position adds little when the uncertainty concerns a small seam, a label or the edge of a sole.

04

Inspect visible condition without promising performance

Review the upper, lining where visible, stitching, fasteners and the join between upper and sole. Look for visible tears, stains, missing parts, unusual gaps or a mismatch between the two shoes. Distinguish a feature of the design from damage by checking the listing reference. If a mark may be lighting or dust, request a close-up before deciding. Photographs can document a visible concern; they cannot determine every material property or how the shoes will perform after repeated wear.

Do not describe a pair as authenticated, durable, waterproof or comfortable simply because its QC pictures look acceptable. A warehouse photograph is not a material test or a long-term wear test. Likewise, a photograph cannot establish smell or accurately reproduce every colour on every screen. Note what is visible and what remains unknown. This keeps the decision useful: you can accept the observed condition while still recognising the limits of the evidence, or ask for a particular check when the service is available.

05

Check soles, closures and included accessories

Inspect the outsole pattern, edges and any visible mismatch between the pair. Check that both shoes have the expected laces, straps, buckles or other closures. If the listing includes extra laces, insoles, tags or another accessory, confirm the agreed contents against the order rather than assuming that every studio photograph shows what is included. Keep a short contents list so that a missing accessory can be explained clearly while the item is still at the warehouse.

Consider this hypothetical case: the pair appears correct from above, but the listing includes a second set of laces and only one set appears in the photographs. The next action is to ask whether the additional laces are inside the box, not to reject the shoes immediately or assume the accessory is present. If the box is needed for that check, postpone its removal. Review the answer and any supporting photograph before closing the concern or making the packing instructions final.

06

Write a photo request that leads to a decision

A strong request identifies the order, the concern and the evidence needed. For example, ask for a close-up of the outer sole join on the right shoe because the existing image appears to show a gap. Or request both heel counters photographed level from the back because the current angle makes alignment unclear. Avoid asking for a complete new photo set when one focused image would answer the question. Keep the original photograph so the agent can locate the area quickly.

Check whether additional photographs or measurements are available and whether there is a charge before ordering the service. Read the return or exchange deadline on the same day. Do not assume that requesting another image extends the time available for after-sales action. If the evidence is still incomplete near the deadline, ask the agent what options remain. Maintain one clear discussion on the existing order rather than scattering different instructions across several channels that may not share the same context.

07

Make an acceptance decision before changing the packaging

Use three practical outcomes for your review: accept the visible condition, request clarification, or ask about the available return or exchange process. A clarification should name the missing information and how it could change your decision. A return request should identify the mismatch or concern and include the relevant evidence. Read the seller and agent conditions rather than assuming that every concern qualifies for a free return or that every cost will be refunded.

Keep the shoes, labels and packaging intact while the decision is open. Removing a box can reduce parcel volume, but it is a separate packing choice with consequences for protection and after-sales handling. Once you accept the pair, review the shoebox guide to compare packaging options against the selected shipping line's measurements and conditions. Do not ask for box removal only because someone else's parcel was cheaper; your contents, destination and actual packed dimensions determine the useful comparison.

08

Keep the evidence through dispatch and delivery

Save the accepted QC images, the order specification and the final packing instructions with the parcel identifier. When several pairs are consolidated, record which orders belong to the parcel so that later tracking or delivery questions can be matched to the correct contents. Check the parcel record for the selected service and any requested protection. An item being stored is not the same as a parcel being dispatched, so keep those events separate in your notes.

When the parcel arrives, compare the received contents with the saved record and document any visible problem before discarding packaging. Contact the relevant service through its current process if something needs investigation. Use the experience to improve your next request: perhaps a size label, accessory photo or clearer heel view would have helped. The aim of this checklist is a timely, evidence-based purchase decision. It complements the general QC guide without turning a set of photographs into a guarantee about authenticity, fit or future performance.
