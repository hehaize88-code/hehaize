Joyagoo Size Guide: Measure Clothing and Shoes Before Buying

Choose clothing and shoe sizes on Joyagoo using seller charts, flat garment measurements and clear warehouse photo requests before international shipping.

01

Choose a size from measurements, not the letter on the label

There is no single Joyagoo size chart that makes every seller's clothing and shoes equivalent. The useful chart belongs to the exact product and variation you are ordering. Start with a garment or pair of shoes that fits you well, record its measurements, and compare those measurements with the listing. A familiar label such as medium or EU 42 is a starting point, not evidence that two unrelated items have the same dimensions.

Make the comparison before paying, while another size or seller is still an easy option. Save the chart together with the product address, selected size and date. Listings can change, and a screenshot without the product identity is difficult to use later. If the seller supplies no measurements, ask the agent whether they can obtain them. Decide how much uncertainty you are comfortable accepting instead of treating an absent chart as permission to use your usual size.

02

Read the chart headings and measurement method

Check whether the figures describe the wearer's body or the finished garment. A chest circumference for a person is not the same as a shirt measured flat across the chest. A flat width of 56 cm corresponds to approximately 112 cm around that part of the garment, but doubling the number does not describe its stretch, shape or room for movement. Use the same measurement method on both the reference garment and the seller's chart.

Confirm centimetres versus inches before comparing numbers. One inch equals 2.54 centimetres; keep the original unit in your notes to avoid repeated rounding. Read any seller tolerance as a possible variation, not as a promise that the delivered item will hit the centre value. If the acceptable range is narrow, request clarification. A chart with several unexplained columns needs an explanation of each column, particularly shoulder width, sleeve length and measurements taken from different starting points.

03

Build a clothing reference that you can reuse

Lay a well-fitting garment flat without stretching it. For a top, record chest width from side to side, body length using a stated starting point, shoulder width if the seams are comparable, and sleeve length. Photograph the tape position so you can repeat the method. A dropped shoulder or curved hem makes a number less useful unless you also describe where the measurement begins and ends. Choose a reference with a similar cut and fabric to the item you want.

For trousers, note the waist measured flat, rise, thigh width and inseam. Separate a relaxed elastic waist from a stretched measurement. A waistband that can expand does not guarantee comfortable room through the hips or thighs. If the reference fits only with a belt or feels restrictive when sitting, record that too. The purpose is to describe a fit you actually want, rather than to collect numbers that look precise but come from an unsuitable comparison item.

04

Compare fit, fabric and layering before choosing between sizes

Two products with similar measurements can feel different because of fabric, lining and construction. A rigid jacket needs a different fit decision from a stretchy base layer. Decide whether you plan to wear a shirt, a thick sweater or nothing bulky underneath. Compare the seller's measurements with your reference while accounting for that intended use. Avoid a universal instruction to size up: increasing one dimension can also make sleeves, shoulders or the overall length unsuitable.

Consider this illustrative comparison. Your reference shirt measures 55 cm across the chest and 72 cm in body length. A listing offers one size at 54 by 71 cm and another at 57 by 74 cm. Neither is automatically correct. The first may feel tighter, while the second may be longer than you want. Check the remaining dimensions, the seller's tolerance and your preferred fit. If neither option meets the important measurements, a different cut can be a better choice than compromising on every dimension.

05

Check shoe sizing with the seller’s own definitions

Record the size system used by the listing and whether it distinguishes men's, women's or children's shoes. US, UK, EU and centimetre labels should not be converted through a random universal table. Prefer the seller's chart for that product and ask what any centimetre figure describes. Foot length, insole length and outsole length are different measurements. An outsole photograph cannot tell you the space available inside the shoe, especially around a tapered toe or thick heel padding.

Measure both feet using the same method and record the larger result, then follow the chart's instructions for how that measurement is used. Do not add a fixed allowance unless the seller explains that it is required. If requesting an insole measurement at the warehouse, ask whether the insole can be removed without damage and how it will be measured. Compare it with a similar well-fitting shoe, while recognising that width, toe shape and internal volume still affect comfort.

06

Use warehouse photos to check the specific order

When QC photographs become available, first match them with the product order and selected variation. Check the size label, colour and quantity before spending time on a detailed measurement. A correctly labelled size may still differ from the listing's measurements, and a photographed label is not a fit test. If one dimension would change your decision, ask for a clear photograph showing the complete tape, its starting point and the relevant garment or shoe in the same frame.

Make the request specific. For example, ask for the shirt laid flat with the tape running horizontally between the underarm seams, without stretching the fabric. For trousers, specify whether you need relaxed waist width or inseam. Avoid asking simply for a better photo when the real problem is an undefined measuring method. Confirm the availability and price of additional services in the account, then check any return deadline while you wait. An unanswered measurement request should not be assumed to pause that deadline.

07

Decide whether to keep, clarify or request a return

Put the ordered specification, seller chart and warehouse evidence next to one another. Separate a visible mismatch, such as the wrong size label, from a preference issue, such as wanting a looser fit than the chart suggested. Keep a short explanation of the problem and attach the relevant photographs to the existing order discussion. This helps the agent understand whether you need a corrected measurement, a seller response or information about the available after-sales options.

Do not remove labels or request disposal of retail packaging while a return decision remains open. Check the current conditions and costs before accepting an exchange, especially if a replacement would arrive after the rest of your parcel is ready. If the evidence is insufficient and the deadline is close, ask for a clear next step rather than waiting indefinitely. A decision made before international packing generally leaves more practical options than discovering a sizing concern after the parcel has been dispatched.

08

Keep a small size record for your next order

Save the final measurements, the size you received and a brief note about fit once the item arrives. Include the seller or product identifier because a successful size from one listing does not establish a rule for every other listing. Record whether the measurement was taken before or after washing, since comparing different conditions can be misleading. Over time, a few reliable reference items are more useful than a long list of unexplained size labels copied from other buyers.

Before submitting your next parcel, check that each item has an acceptance decision and that every unresolved measurement has a named follow-up. Use the general QC checklist for quantity and visible condition, and the shoe QC guide for pair-specific checks. Browse the spreadsheet by product name or source ID only after you know which measurements matter. Product discovery finds candidates; the size comparison determines whether a candidate is suitable for you.
