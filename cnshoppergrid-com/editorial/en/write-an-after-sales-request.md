# How to write a CNShopper after-sales request that identifies the problem

An effective after-sales request lets another person reconstruct the purchase, see what went wrong and understand what decision you need. The evidence changes depending on whether the goods are with the seller, in the agent's warehouse or already at your address. CNShopper's ordering and help pages give context for those stages. Check the options available on your own order before asking for a remedy. Availability, timing and costs can depend on the seller, service and circumstances.

## Identify the exact purchase line

Open the original order and copy the product title, seller option text, quantity and line reference. Include the warehouse or international parcel reference if the problem happened later. A single shipment can contain several nearly identical products. If you report that “the black one is wrong,” the agent has to guess which seller or purchase you mean. Spell out the distinguishing detail in the first sentence, such as the black single-pocket organizer from the particular purchase line you identify.

Explain the agreed specification using the record from the time of purchase. Sellers can change photographs and option names on a live page. A saved selection, confirmation message or dated screenshot can show the original wording. If you supplied special instructions, include the agent's response along with your own message. A request to choose the longer model and an acknowledgement that the seller could supply it are two different pieces of evidence. Keep their roles clear.

## Describe one observable discrepancy

State what you expected, what you can see and where the observation came from. “The selected option says two inner pockets. The inside photograph of this warehouse item shows one stitched pocket” gives the agent something to inspect. “Bad quality” could mean an appearance issue, incorrect material, poor workmanship or a damaged shipment. When the difference cannot be seen in a photo, describe the practical symptom and the conditions under which you observed it.

A fit concern needs different evidence from a wrong-size shipment. Check the sewn-in label and how the seller defines the listed measurements. A garment that feels tight can carry the exact ordered size. If a seller gave a garment width, show which two points you measured and whether the fabric was stretched. If you only have a size label, report that observation honestly. Ask whether another measurement or photograph is available if it would help establish the distinction.

## Choose photographs that can answer a question

Use an overall image to orient the person reviewing the case, then add a closer image of the disputed detail. For a color mismatch, photograph both the product and any option label if they are available. Describe the lighting when color is central to the decision. Screen settings, warehouse lighting and the original listing image can all affect appearance. A label with the seller's color code can give the reviewer a second way to identify what was sent.

For a damaged delivery, keep the packaging while the issue is being assessed. Photograph the outside condition and the item's location or protective material if you can do so at the time of opening. Describe what you directly observed. A crushed corner on a box and a scratch on the goods can both be documented without deciding who caused the damage. If you noticed the scratch later, say when you found it. That detail helps the reviewer interpret the evidence fairly.

## Match the request to the item's location

If the item is still in the warehouse, decide whether you want more information before an exchange or return. The issue may be resolved by a clearer image of a label or a count of pieces inside a bundle. Specify the warehouse item and ask whether the check involves opening sealed packaging or paying a fee. Keep it out of a parcel submission while you are deciding. Once the item is packed with unrelated goods, identifying and separating it may require a different process.

If you already received the item overseas, describe the delivery and opening dates as accurately as possible. Read the service information attached to the parcel and ask where to submit the relevant documents. Do not send an unrequested international return to an address copied from a domestic label. Ask for the authorized recipient, required reference, method and who would pay each leg before arranging any shipment. Keep written instructions together with the case so future tracking can be matched to the correct request.

## Ask for a specific remedy and its conditions

Choose the practical result you need. An additional inspection, exchange for a named option, return assessment or clarification of a charge each requires a different response. When requesting an exchange, specify what to do if the replacement is unavailable. A second-choice color you mentioned casually earlier can be mistaken for authorization. Give a clear instruction to pause if a replacement would change the size, design, price or another feature that affects your decision.

If money is involved, ask for the amount, currency and components before agreeing. A seller refund, domestic return postage, a service fee and international freight may appear in different records. Ask which amounts the proposed resolution addresses and where a credit would appear. When comparing the proposal with your original payment, use the affected line. A payment that covered several products is an unreliable measure of the cost associated with a single defective item.

## Put the evidence in an order the reviewer can follow

Start with your request, then identify the item and give a short sequence of events. For example, explain that you ordered a particular organizer, a warehouse photo shows a different fastening, and you would like the agent to check the seller option before international packing. Follow with the purchase reference, the original option wording and two labeled images. The reviewer can now inspect the records in the same order you used to identify the issue.

Attach private identifying information only through the official support channel when necessary. A parcel reference and purchase line often provide a cleaner starting point than a full screenshot of your account, balance and delivery address. Keep originals of images that you crop for a message. If support requests a wider view of the package or the complete label, you can share the requested version through the appropriate channel without losing your original context.

## Record the response and check that it was carried out

Save the date and wording of a proposed resolution before approving it. If an exchange is offered, verify whether the proposal covers the return to the seller, the replacement purchase and its delivery back to the warehouse. If clarification is still needed, reply within the existing case when possible and list the one point preventing a decision. Repeating the initial complaint in a new thread can separate useful photographs from the answer they prompted.

Finally, compare the actual result with what you approved. For an exchange, inspect the new warehouse entry against the corrected option. For a refund, find the associated transaction and check its amount and currency against the agreement. For a shipping claim, keep the parcel reference with the final message. Close your notes with what happened and what remains outstanding. That final comparison catches cases where a seller accepted a return but the replacement or credit still needs to be identified.

If several identical units were purchased, specify which units the outcome covers. A credit offered for one damaged piece does not settle a separate quantity discrepancy on the same purchase. Keep one line per issue in your notes, even if support handles both in a single case. That small distinction makes the final comparison with the agent's response much easier.
