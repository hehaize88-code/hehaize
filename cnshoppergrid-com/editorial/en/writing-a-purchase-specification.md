# Writing a manual purchase specification a CNShopper buyer can actually use
A useful manual purchase specification answers a practical question: could someone who has never seen your spreadsheet order the correct unit from the correct seller using only your message? If the answer depends on recognizing a familiar image, improve the specification before sending it. A short, unambiguous instruction is more effective than several paragraphs of preferences that contradict the selected option.

## Name the item in a way that survives translation
Start with the seller's product URL and the exact original option wording. Add a plain-language description in your own language. These fields serve different functions. The original wording helps the purchaser select the right seller control. Your explanation tells the purchaser why the choice matters. One should not silently replace the other.

Imagine a listing for a lunchbox in which the options translate as “standard,” “enhanced” and “portable.” The word “portable” sounds as though it could describe every lunchbox. The seller's original option may identify a specific two-tier version with a carrying handle. Record the original text, a screenshot of the selection and a description such as “two stackable compartments with handle.” If the seller page and your interpretation disagree, ask the question before commissioning the purchase.

## Specify the unit before the quantity
Define what counts as one ordered unit. A stationery listing may sell one pencil, one pack of twelve or one display box of packs. The photograph can depict all three at once. A quantity of two means very different things depending on which selector is active. Write the selected unit alongside the desired number and the expected physical total.

For example, “two packs of twelve blank index cards, twenty-four cards total” can be checked at purchase and on arrival. “Two index cards sets” leaves uncertainty about both packaging and count. If the seller's minimum requires a larger quantity than you want, ask whether the combination you need is available at the stated price. Do not increase the form quantity until you understand what the minimum applies to.

## Separate essential attributes from preferences
Give the purchaser a decisive description of what must match. Essential attributes might include dimensions, colour code, plug type, material, personalization text or an included accessory. A preference is something you would trade away for another benefit. Put those in a separate sentence and avoid giving permission to improvise unless you intend to accept the result.

Consider a desk tray that must fit a 32-centimetre shelf. External width is essential; the colour of a removable divider might be flexible. An instruction saying “32 cm tray preferred, grey please” obscures the priority. Write “outside width must be no more than 32 cm; ask before purchase if the seller gives only inside width. Grey divider preferred; another divider colour is acceptable.” This lets the purchaser decline a visually similar tray that would not physically fit.

## Attach the right view of the listing
Save a screenshot that includes the product identity, selected option and price. If you need to send two screenshots, make it clear that they belong to the same listing. A highly cropped photo of a blue swatch can distinguish a colour on your own screen while revealing nothing about which seller, product or version it describes.

For customized goods, capture the written instruction separately from a design mock-up. A mock-up can show placement while failing to establish spelling, capitalization or spacing. Type the text exactly as required and specify any limit that matters, such as one line on the outer face. Ask whether the seller can confirm the design before production if that affects your decision. Do not assume an ordinary return opportunity applies to a custom piece; read the applicable seller and agent conditions.

## State what to do if the seller cannot fulfil the request
A specification should contain an exit condition. If the precise option is missing, would you like a message, a cancellation proposal or another quote? Give permission only for the substitutions you would actually accept. “Please contact me” is useful when a wrong alternative would leave you with an unusable item. For low-priority attributes, define a narrow substitute explicitly.

Suppose you need two notebooks with identical ruling for a course. Either blue or grey covers are acceptable, but one blank book and one lined book are not. Write the acceptable cover colours and the essential ruling for both. If the seller offers one lined blue and one blank grey, the purchaser can recognize that the pair fails your requirement even though both cover colours were permitted.

## Make the budget condition measurable
Record the displayed price for the chosen option and the currency. If your instruction contains a maximum, say what it includes. A ceiling for the goods alone differs from a ceiling that includes seller-to-warehouse delivery. Keep international freight separate unless you have an actual quote for the packed parcel. CNShopper's ordering guide treats purchase confirmation and international shipping as different stages.

For example, a buyer might approve one unit at 80 in the seller's currency and domestic delivery up to 10. If the merchant changes the unit price to 85 and delivery to 5, the total is still 90, but the treatment depends on what the buyer authorized. A combined ceiling allows a different decision from separate ceilings. Express your own condition in the form that matters to you and verify what CNShopper can act on before the order proceeds. These example figures do not represent platform fees.

## Use a read-back test on the assembled note
Read the request as if the original photos are unavailable. Can you identify the source seller? Which option would you click? How many units would you buy? Does the listing show the most important requirement, or do you need the merchant to confirm it? Which change requires asking the buyer? If any of these questions has two plausible answers, rewrite that portion.

A compact request might read: “Seller link attached. Choose option with the original wording shown in screenshot A. One two-tier green lunchbox with carrying handle; one unit. Both compartments must be included. Ask the seller whether the listed width includes the handle. Please pause if outside width exceeds the marked shelf limit or if the merchant proposes another version.” Add the actual amount, currency and references in the order. This example illustrates structure; the fields on the current purchase form remain the place to enter the transaction details.

## Use the same specification when reviewing the result
Preserve the accepted version of the note with your order reference. At warehouse arrival, compare the visible item with the essential attributes first. A front view may confirm colour and general shape. A photograph of the opened lunchbox can confirm the second compartment. A tape view across the widest point can address the shelf-width requirement. Ask only for the additional evidence that changes your decision, checking the available service before expecting a particular inspection.

If the item differs, report the exact requested attribute, the evidence of what arrived and the action you want considered. Keep the original requirement distinct from a preference added after purchase. A clean specification helps both sides understand whether the received unit conflicts with the order or whether you have changed your mind. It remains useful long after the product has disappeared from the seller's live menu.

Keep a dated copy of the final specification after any edits. If the first message requested blue and the seller later offered navy, record whether you accepted the change and which item it applied to. A reply that says only “yes” makes sense within one conversation but becomes ambiguous when several purchase lines are reviewed together. Copy the accepted seller wording into the final record.
