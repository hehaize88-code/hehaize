# A size is unavailable on CNShopper: how to compare a substitute before agreeing
A selected size can disappear between saving a spreadsheet item and asking an agent to buy it. The useful response depends on what is missing: the seller may be out of stock, the imported selector may omit a valid option, or two sizing systems may be displayed differently. Establish which situation applies before accepting another size or telling the purchaser to cancel.

## Recreate the exact combination
Return to the current seller listing. Select the intended colour, model or cut first, then inspect sizes for that combination. A medium available in a blue regular fit does not establish that a medium in a black short fit is available. Save the full original option text and the current view. Compare it with your earlier note and the order you submitted.

If the size appears on the seller page but is missing from the imported purchase screen, report the discrepancy with the seller link and two clear screenshots. Ask whether the exact option can be confirmed. If it is absent from both screens, ask the merchant about availability through the appropriate channel. A seller estimate for a future restock is different from a confirmed unit available for purchase now. Record which kind of answer you received.

## Identify the fit requirement behind the label
A label such as M or EU 42 is a shorthand; it does not describe every relevant dimension. Determine what made the original size attractive. For trousers, you might need a particular waist range, rise, thigh width and inseam. For a shirt, chest width, shoulder position, sleeve length and overall length may matter independently. Write down the limiting measurement rather than assuming one size up or down will work.

Choose a comparison garment with a similar construction that fits for the same use. A loose sweatshirt for layering and a tailored jacket worn over a shirt do not serve as interchangeable baselines. Lay the comparison garment as the seller's diagram instructs and record units and endpoints. A waist circumference on a size table cannot be compared directly to a relaxed flat width without checking how both were measured.

## Confirm what the proposed alternative actually is
Ask for the exact seller option, not just the translated words “similar size.” A proposed replacement might be the next size of the same garment, the same label in another cut, a different colour that remains in stock or a different listing entirely. Each requires a different comparison. If the seller offers a substitute from another listing, obtain its own chart, photos, price and conditions.

Imagine a jacket unavailable in short medium. The alternatives are regular medium and short large. Regular medium may keep a similar chest width while adding body length. Short large may retain the intended length while increasing width and shoulder span. Neither is universally “closer.” State which dimensions you need to preserve and compare both alternatives with those requirements. If the necessary measurements are absent, the correct conclusion is uncertainty rather than a confident size recommendation.

## Read size charts as measurements, not conversion tables
Look for the measurement method, units, garment position and whether the numbers describe the item or the wearer. A body chart showing recommended chest circumference has a different meaning from the actual finished garment circumference. An elastic waistband measured flat and relaxed can differ substantially from its stretched width. Ask for clarification when a term such as “waist” appears without a diagram.

For an illustrative example, your own jacket measures 54 centimetres flat underarm to underarm. A seller chart lists chest 108. The figures might describe similar garment widths if 108 is the complete garment circumference using comparable endpoints. They are not necessarily equivalent if 108 is a recommended wearer measurement. This arithmetic only identifies the question to ask. It does not prove that the substitute will fit, because cut, sleeve shape and fabric behaviour can also differ.

## Decide whether a measurement could change your answer
If the seller can provide a measurement before purchase, identify the one or two details that would resolve the uncertainty. “What are all the dimensions?” may generate another generic chart. “For the short large in black, what is the centre-back length from collar seam to hem?” identifies an exact option and measurement. Ask whether the current stock is the version represented by the chart.

If the only available measurement comes after warehouse intake, decide whether the purchase risk and current return conditions are acceptable before ordering. CNShopper describes warehouse photographs as a visual review and notes that additional inspection can depend on availability. An expectation that later photos will answer your question is not the same as a confirmed seller commitment to supply a suitable size or accept a return.

## Give the purchaser an explicit decision
Once you choose, send an instruction that names the original order and the precise action. If you accept the substitute, specify the replacement seller option, quantity, confirmed price and any required condition. If you decline it, say whether you want the order held for clarification or cancelled according to the options actually available. Avoid mixing “try the large” with “only buy medium” in the same message.

For example, “The requested black short M is unavailable. Please order black short L only if the seller confirms the back length at the agreed measurement. If it exceeds that figure, contact me before purchase.” If you do not want a conditional purchase, use a simpler decision and wait for the answer first. Keep the purchaser's confirmation alongside your message so it is clear which instruction was accepted.

## Compare the warehouse item with the revised order
At intake, review the accepted replacement specification, not merely the first item saved to your spreadsheet. If you approved short large, a large label can be correct even though the original wishlist said medium. However, the label alone may not confirm the cut or measurement that justified the change. Request the appropriate view for the remaining question if the service permits it.

Keep the photo linked to the specific order. Two black jackets photographed on the same surface can be easy to confuse. Look for the option label, an overall view and the relevant measurement. If the received item differs from the revised confirmation, describe that exact difference to support while the unit is available for inspection. The original request and the accepted substitution provide the context for the discrepancy.

## Preserve the original outcome for future purchases
Save a short result such as “short medium unavailable; accepted short large after checking back length; actual warehouse measurement recorded.” If you declined a substitute, keep the reason, such as excessive length or incompatible rise. Those notes improve future purchases from the same seller without pretending that a size label has universal meaning across shops.

The decision is complete when you can identify the actual available option, explain why its measurements do or do not meet your need and point to the instruction the purchaser received. A different size can be reasonable, but its suitability should follow from the garment and the use you have in mind. It should never depend solely on a familiar letter, a similar thumbnail or a hurried suggestion to choose the next option.

Keep a record of the option you declined as well as the one you approved. If a seller offers both a different color in the requested size and the requested color in a different size, a reply referring to the “second option” can become unclear when messages are translated or forwarded. Repeat the accepted color, size and model in full, and request confirmation on the affected purchase line.
