PikoBuy Jacket Spreadsheet: Compare Fit, Lining and Weight
Build a PikoBuy jacket shortlist with clear garment measurements, lining claims, closure checks and separate product and parcel weights.
Jacket finds

A useful PikoBuy jacket spreadsheet makes fit, construction and parcel implications easy to compare before you choose a listing. Jackets create particular problems because the same size label can cover a fitted coat, a loose overshirt or a bulky insulated layer. This guide explains how to record those differences, plan useful warehouse photos and keep unsupported warmth or fabric claims out of the comparison.

The catalogue examples are existing records, not newly inspected jackets. The site's July 30, 2026 snapshot includes a Celine Coat and a Louis Vuitton Jacket entry. Names and photographs are listing identifiers, not proof of authenticity or material composition. Their destination routes may open catalogue searches where an exact listing needs to be selected again. Check the item and option before relying on any historical record.

## Choose the jacket's job before its size label

Define the intended use in practical terms: an outer layer over a thin shirt, room for a hoodie, a short casual jacket or a longer coat. Then choose a garment you already own that works for that use. The comparison garment gives you a concrete starting point for length, chest, sleeve and shoulder measurements. It does not guarantee that another construction will fit identically.

Record layering needs separately from body measurements. A jacket's outside chest width includes fabric, lining and possibly insulation, so the usable space inside may differ from a light shirt with the same number. Avoid inventing a universal amount of extra room. Compare the seller's defined measurements, your reference garment and the construction visible in the listing, then mark anything that still needs clarification.

## Preserve one exact listing and configuration

Save the final product address, product identifier, displayed seller, model wording and date. Select the exact colour, size and version before recording price. Some pages combine a lightweight and lined version, several lengths or garments with different closures. The main image can show one version while the cheapest option belongs to another. Keep the selected text with the image that represents it.

PikoBuy's official beginner guide asks buyers to confirm specifications, colour and size before submitting the purchasing order. Apply that instruction literally. If you discover a replacement link, make a new record and preserve the old one as a reference. Do not assume that a similar promotional image means the new seller offers the same jacket, measurements, lining or return terms.

## Measure the jacket with named endpoints

Start with chest width, body length, shoulder width and sleeve length when the seller defines them. For a conventional set-in sleeve, a shoulder-seam-to-cuff measurement differs from a raglan measurement taken from the centre back. A length measured from the collar's top differs from one beginning at the high shoulder point. Record the method rather than comparing numbers that happen to share a unit.

Measure your reference garment with the same closure state and endpoints. Note whether it is zipped or buttoned, laid flat and relaxed, and whether a hood or collar is excluded. Keep centimetres and inches in separate labelled fields if you need both. Convert units consistently, but do not round an uncertain fit into apparent certainty. A measurement tolerance applies only when the seller actually provides it.

## Treat lining and insulation as separate questions

Create separate fields for outer-fabric claim, lining claim and insulation claim. A shiny interior does not prove a particular fibre, and a bulky silhouette does not establish fill composition or warmth. Preserve percentages, fill wording and care instructions exactly when the listing supplies them. If the source only says “winter” or “warm,” record that as marketing language without inventing a temperature rating.

Ask which version the material information describes. A product page can reuse text across colours or constructions. Look for an image of the inside, seams and care label that belongs to the selected option. Warehouse photos may show the printed label and visible construction, but they cannot independently verify laboratory properties, water resistance, breathability or the accuracy of a fibre percentage.

## Build a short jacket-specific QC request

Confirm identity first: chosen colour, size label, closure type, collar or hood shape, pocket arrangement and included removable pieces. Then inspect visible condition: tears, stains, detached seams, damaged fasteners and obvious panel mismatches. Request a clear front and back view before narrowing in on a zipper, cuff or lining detail. A close-up loses value if the whole garment's identity remains uncertain.

Choose measurements that could change the decision. A sleeve request should name its start and end, and a chest request should specify the jacket's closure state. If a removable lining or hood is advertised, verify its presence without assuming a photograph proves its future performance. Avoid requesting destructive handling. PikoBuy's published guidance supports photo confirmation and defect checking, not an unlimited specialist inspection service.

## Use the existing catalogue entries carefully

The Celine Coat record is a prompt to check coat-specific fields: overall length, front overlap or closure, sleeve method and whether the displayed lining belongs to the chosen option. The Louis Vuitton Jacket record is a prompt to identify its exact version, pocket layout and any pattern alignment that matters to you. Those are inspection questions, not claims about either product's quality.

Open the destination and rebuild the selected-option record if it returns several results. A search result is not a confirmed match. Do not transfer a historical price, another product's photographs or a care-label claim onto a visually similar item. If the original item cannot be identified, keep its record as unavailable and select a new candidate with its own evidence trail.

## Separate garment weight from packed dimensions

A jacket's weight can influence a parcel, but it is not a quality score. Record whether a figure comes from seller text, a warehouse scale or a planning estimate. Keep the selected size and whether packaging is included beside the number. Do not compare a bare garment weight with another item's packed weight and conclude that one construction is heavier or better.

PikoBuy's official shipping estimator asks for weight and length, width and height alongside destination and product type. A bulky jacket can alter a parcel's dimensions even if its item weight appears manageable. Compare actual packing options when they become available. Do not promise a fixed saving from compression, and consider whether the garment's shape, finish or hardware requires protective handling.

## Compare two candidates on the same basis

Consider a hypothetical coat whose seller chart defines shoulder-to-hem length and a hypothetical jacket whose chart only gives “length.” The first has clearer evidence, but that alone does not make it the better garment. Clarify the second method before comparing fit. Likewise, a seller's precise lining statement is still a seller statement; it should not become a verified material claim in your table.

Make the final comparison across intended use, exact option, measurement methods, visible construction, selected price and known packing constraints. Mark unknowns explicitly. A candidate can remain worth considering while one answer is pending. Reject a listing when an essential requirement cannot be resolved, rather than compensating for that gap with a lower price or a more attractive photo.

## Decide before the return window becomes urgent

Review warehouse evidence soon after it becomes available. Compare it with the exact configuration you saved, then separate wrong-item or visible-damage issues from a change of preference. Preserve the photos and option record if you request help. Read the order's current return eligibility before asking for labels, packaging or detachable pieces to be discarded. Keeping those options available can matter more than a small preliminary packing saving.
