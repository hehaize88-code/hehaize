PikoBuy Bag Spreadsheet: Dimensions, Hardware and QC

A useful bag row has to describe more than a style name and a product photo. It should preserve the exact option, measurable dimensions, hardware configuration, interior layout, included pieces and the evidence needed at the warehouse. This guide turns a bag find into a decision record without claiming that photos can prove materials, authenticity or long-term durability.

Define the exact bag before comparing prices

Begin with fields that identify the live offer: marketplace, seller name, product URL, listing ID, original title and the date checked. Then record the complete option path. A listing may combine several sizes, colours, materials, strap sets or hardware finishes under one headline. The thumbnail price often belongs to only one branch. Save the price after selecting the intended branch, and keep the option wording exactly as displayed beside a short English note.

Create separate columns for bag type, stated size, colour, hardware colour, strap type, closure, included pouch or accessories and quantity. If a choice is unclear, mark it unknown. Do not fill a blank cell from a promotional image. PikoBuy's current beginner guide tells buyers to confirm specifications, colour and size before submitting an order. A spreadsheet should preserve those same decision fields so the imported order can be checked against the source.

Record whether the offer is live, unavailable, replaced or awaiting an answer. A live flag means the exact option was visible on the review date; it does not certify stock, seller reliability or product quality.

Make every dimension reproducible

Bag dimensions are useful only when the endpoints are clear. Store length, height and depth in separate columns with units and the source of each value. Note whether a dimension comes from the seller chart, a diagram, written copy or a warehouse measurement. For a soft bag, record whether the measurement appears relaxed, filled or flattened. If handles are included in a height figure, say so.

Do not average conflicting values. Keep the seller chart, a later message and warehouse evidence in separate cells. A difference may come from measurement method, a different size option or normal shape variation. The right response is to expose the conflict and ask which value belongs to the selected variant.

Add capacity questions that dimensions alone cannot answer: laptop sleeve size, opening width, interior divider, pocket count and whether a rigid object must fit. Treat those as requirements, not assumptions. A product that looks large in a styled image can still have a narrow opening or divided interior.

Record hardware as a configuration

Hardware deserves its own block because it affects both identity and visible-condition review. Useful fields include closure type, zipper count, clasp shape, buckle finish, feet, rings, chain or strap attachments and any removable pieces. Record what the selected option promises and what is merely shown in an image. If several colours appear, attach the observation to the exact variant.

At the warehouse, request evidence that can show whether the expected components are present, aligned and visibly undamaged. Front, back, base, side and interior views can establish the broad configuration. Close views may help reveal scratches, missing screws, broken teeth, bent fittings, uneven plating or loose attachments. They cannot establish metal composition, coating life, load capacity or resistance to repeated use.

Write acceptance rules before the photos arrive. For example: correct hardware colour, both strap clips present, zipper path visually continuous and no obvious deep scratch on the front closure. Prewritten rules reduce the temptation to accept a mismatch simply because returning it takes effort.

Map the interior and included pieces

Interior layout is often the difference between a usable bag and a disappointing one. Add fields for lining colour, main compartments, zipped pockets, slip pockets, dividers and removable inserts. If the listing includes a dust bag, shoulder strap, charm, lock, key pouch or other accessory, list each item rather than writing “full set.” That phrase has no stable meaning across sellers.

Packaging should be recorded separately from product contents. A box may affect parcel volume, while protective stuffing can help preserve shape. PikoBuy's guide says buyers may request minimal or reinforced packaging, but the best choice depends on the specific product and current options. Record what protection is required, what can be removed and which packaging must stay intact while any return decision remains open.

For structured bags, note the parts most vulnerable to pressure: corners, handles, base, opening and rigid panels. For soft bags, note whether folding is acceptable. This produces an actionable packing instruction instead of a generic request for “safe shipping.”

Build a warehouse evidence sequence

Start the QC record with identity: correct colour, selected size, intended hardware finish, strap set and included pieces. Next compare proportions and major construction from consistent views. Review seam paths, edge finishing, handle attachment, panel symmetry, pocket alignment, closure position and visible surface damage. If a requirement cannot be seen, mark it not verified instead of converting a seller claim into a warehouse fact.

Use measurements only where they affect the decision. A request should name the exact endpoints and units, such as base width from outside edge to outside edge or opening width at its narrowest usable point. Save the image date and observed reading. A tape placed at a different angle should not silently replace the seller's method.

PikoBuy's public shipping terms say inspection photos can be reviewed and additional detailed photos may be requested, while some professional or special products cannot receive professional inspection. Plan a concise shot list around the buyer's acceptance rules rather than assuming unlimited evidence or specialist testing.

Compare two hypothetical bag listings

Suppose Listing A gives three dimensions, a removable strap and two hardware finishes, while Listing B is cheaper but combines two sizes in one image and does not identify the included strap. Create two rows. Preserve both IDs, seller names and selected option prices. Mark A's dimensions as seller-stated and attach them to its exact size. Mark B's size and strap as unresolved.

  1. Compare only the dimensions tied to the intended variant.
  2. Check whether the opening and interior layout satisfy the real use case.
  3. Ask Listing B one precise question about size and included strap.
  4. Define visible QC priorities for hardware, corners, handles and contents.
  5. Estimate packaging sensitivity separately from product price.
  6. Keep both rows in research until the must-have fields are resolved.

The cheaper listing is not automatically the better find. The stronger row is the one that connects a clear configuration to measurable requirements, observable warehouse evidence and an intentional packing decision.

Use a three-gate bag decision

Configuration gate: the exact size, colour, hardware, strap and included pieces are identified. Use gate: the dimensions, opening and interior layout meet the intended purpose with acceptable unknowns. Evidence gate: the warehouse record can confirm identity and important visible condition without pretending to prove hidden materials or future performance.

Mark the row order-ready only when all three pass. Keep it in research when one seller answer or matching measurement can resolve the choice. Reject it when the required configuration is unavailable, critical dimensions cannot be tied to the selected variant or a must-have component remains ambiguous.

This method makes a PikoBuy bag spreadsheet useful after the listing changes. It retains what was selected, what was measured, what arrived and why the buyer accepted or rejected the item.
