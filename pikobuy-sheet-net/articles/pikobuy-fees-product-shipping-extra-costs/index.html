PikoBuy Fees Explained: Product, Shipping and Extra Costs

Understand PikoBuy fees, the published 8% forwarding service fee, payment stages and optional costs with a worked budget and a practical checkout checklist.

Build the budget around two payments

A PikoBuy spreadsheet price is a starting point for product research. It does not tell you what an international parcel will cost. Before ordering, separate the payment for the selected goods from the later payment for shipping. Keep currency conversion, optional services and any destination charges visible as separate lines. This makes a small product price useful without turning it into a misleading delivered-price promise.

The official beginner guide describes selecting specifications, colour, size and quantity before the first payment. Warehouse check-in and photos come later, followed by parcel submission, route selection and shipping payment. Your budget should follow that sequence. An item marked paid has not necessarily been packed, handed to a carrier or paid for internationally. Record which stage each amount belongs to before comparing totals.

This independent guide explains a budgeting method. Policy details were checked on October 16, 2026; actual charges depend on the current order and the terms shown when you pay. Examples below are invented arithmetic, not live PikoBuy quotations or claimed customer purchases.

Read the 8% forwarding fee in its proper context

The parcel-forwarding section of PikoBuy's user agreement publishes a platform service fee equal to 8% of the shipping fee. It also notes that currency movements and settlement timing can affect the actual calculation. The stated basis is shipping, so applying 8% to the value of every product would misrepresent that clause.

The same agreement describes purchasing-agent arrangements separately and allows necessary service charges to appear at payment confirmation. It is therefore unsafe to label the whole platform permanently fee-free or to assume one percentage covers every service. Read the actual payment breakdown and ask support to identify an unclear line before authorising the transaction.

For a hypothetical shipping base of 300 CNY, eight percent is 24 CNY. If the checkout lists those amounts separately, their subtotal is 324 CNY. If a displayed total already includes the service charge, adding another 24 CNY would count it twice. Save both the wording and the amounts; a screenshot of one final number cannot establish what is included.

Record the selected product price, not the cover price

Choose the exact variation before copying a price into your spreadsheet. A listing can contain different sizes, colours, quantities or versions. Its lowest displayed number may refer to an accessory or another option. Record the seller link, selected option, number of units, displayed currency and date together. Keep domestic seller-to-warehouse delivery separate when it is charged separately.

Use one row per selected item. If two items share a domestic-delivery charge, record that once at order level or allocate it with a stated method. Do not put the full shared charge into both rows and then add the rows. Likewise, an account top-up is a movement of money into the account, not automatically a second purchase expense alongside the order it pays for.

Before the first payment, compare the live order fields with your saved row. Check quantity, unit price and subtotal. Leave unknown amounts blank or explicitly marked pending; entering zero tells a future reader that the item is free. A sensible budget makes uncertainty visible while there is still time to change the shortlist.

Estimate shipping with the parcel's real inputs

The official shipping estimator asks for destination, product type, weight and three parcel dimensions. Use it for planning, then update the inputs after the warehouse measures the packed parcel. Clothing weight alone cannot describe the box, protective material or space taken by a bulky item. Keep estimated product weight distinct from measured parcel weight.

Compare routes using the same destination and parcel assumptions. Check which weight basis the displayed route uses and whether size limits or item restrictions apply. Do not import a divisor or price from another carrier and present it as PikoBuy's universal rule. An unavailable route is not a valid cheap alternative, however attractive an old screenshot looks.

Make a second packing scenario only when that packing option is actually available and suitable for the goods. Record what protection is removed or added. A lower apparent shipping charge is not an automatic improvement if it leaves a fragile item unprotected. The shipping article linked below develops this comparison without claiming a permanent price per kilogram.

Keep optional services and payment costs explicit

Extra photos, packing requests and other optional services should each have a description, selection state and quoted amount. Do not assume they are compulsory or free. The public shipping policy describes additional detailed photos, but this guide does not infer a permanent photo price or a fixed standard-photo allowance from that statement.

If the payment screen offers more than one currency or payment method, compare the actual amount you will be debited and the currency credited to the order. Record any clearly disclosed payment charge separately. An exchange-rate difference is not automatically a named platform fee; label what you can observe rather than assigning a cause you cannot verify.

Destination-related costs depend on the shipment and the current rules that apply to it. This article supplies no universal tax rate or duty-free promise. Check the live route terms and the relevant official destination guidance before committing. A route label alone is not enough to conclude that every possible charge is included.

Work through an illustrative parcel budget

Suppose two selected items cost 120 and 180 CNY. Their combined product subtotal is 300 CNY. Add a hypothetical domestic-delivery charge of 12 CNY: the purchase-stage subtotal becomes 312 CNY. Later, suppose the shipping base is 300 CNY, its separately shown forwarding fee is 24 CNY, and a selected packing service costs 10 CNY. That shipping-stage subtotal is 334 CNY.

The known combined amount is therefore 646 CNY: 312 plus 334. Payment conversion and any unresolved destination costs are still outside that example. These invented values explain the calculation; they do not suggest that a particular weight, country or route currently costs 300 CNY to ship. Replace every input with your own dated quote.

Now imagine the parcel screen changes after repacking. Recalculate the shipping base and any linked service charge from the new breakdown instead of subtracting an assumed saving from 646. Keep the earlier version for comparison. This lets you explain the change using evidence rather than concluding that the platform has added a hidden fee whenever the final amount differs from a planning estimate.

Compare choices without double-counting

A useful comparison has columns for selected goods, domestic delivery, international shipping, platform service charge, optional services, payment costs and unresolved destination costs. Mark each line as quoted, paid or pending. Include a note showing whether the shipping total already contains the platform charge. Keep all calculations in a consistent currency before comparing alternatives.

For several people sharing one parcel, choose an allocation rule before paying. Equal shares, measured weight shares and item-value shares produce different outcomes. None is automatically the carrier's billing method. Show the shared parcel total first, then the agreed division, so an allocated estimate is not mistaken for a separate shipping quote for each item.

Preserve refunds and adjustments as separate entries rather than erasing the original payment. This makes it possible to reconcile what left the payment account, what was credited back and what remains committed to the order. Avoid collecting sensitive card or account details in a shared spreadsheet.

Check the final breakdown before confirming

At purchase confirmation, verify the selected item, quantity, seller and domestic shipping. At parcel confirmation, verify the destination, packed measurements, route, optional services and fee inclusion. Resolve unexplained lines through the current order before paying. Save the resulting receipt and date so the final record describes a transaction rather than an estimate.

Use the fee guide alongside the shipping comparison and the warehouse-photo routine. A good budget does not prove product quality, and a good QC photograph does not fix an unsuitable shipping choice. Keeping those decisions connected gives the spreadsheet a practical purpose: it helps you decide what to buy, what to inspect and what total you are prepared to pay.
