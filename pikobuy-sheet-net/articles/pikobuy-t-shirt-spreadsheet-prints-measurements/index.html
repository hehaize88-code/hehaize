PikoBuy T-Shirt Spreadsheet: Prints, Measurements and Prices

Compare PikoBuy T-shirt finds with exact design options, garment measurements, print details and dated price records rather than size labels alone.

A PikoBuy T-shirt spreadsheet should help you distinguish the design you want from the many options that share a product page. The useful record combines a specific print, colour, sleeve type, measurement basis and selected price. It also states what remains unknown. This guide turns those fields into a practical comparison without pretending that a seller photograph proves fabric quality, wash durability or authenticity.

The examples come from this site's existing T-shirt category, which also contains long-sleeve tops. The July 30, 2026 catalogue snapshot lists Acne Studios Longsleeve and Polo Ralph Lauren Long Sleeve. They are historical discovery references, not newly checked stock. Their names describe catalogue entries. Follow the destination, identify the current product and confirm that the exact option still matches before using either record in an order.

Start with the exact design and sleeve type

Write down the essential design features: short or long sleeves, front graphic, back graphic, colour and approximate silhouette. A product menu may mix several prints or show a complete collection under one title. Preserve the selected design code or option text instead of relying on a translated name alone. Save the matching image with its source and date so that the order can be checked later.

Separate the intended garment from accessories or bundled pieces displayed in the same photograph. Confirm whether the price covers one top, a set or another variation. PikoBuy's public beginner guide describes selecting specifications, colour, size and quantity before payment. A useful T-shirt row captures that complete selection. If an option changes, recheck the price and photographs rather than keeping the previous row unchanged.

Compare measurements instead of familiar size letters

Choose a T-shirt you already wear and measure it laid flat. Useful fields include chest width, body length, shoulder width, sleeve length and hem width when those measurements matter to the fit. State the endpoints and garment condition. A chest width measured across the front is not a body circumference, and doubling it is a calculation that needs a label rather than an invisible assumption.

Check the seller's method before comparing. Body length may begin at the shoulder, collar seam or another point. Sleeve measurements on a dropped-shoulder top cannot be compared directly with a regular sleeve unless the reference points match. For long sleeves, note whether the cuff is relaxed and whether the garment is stretched. Leave an ambiguous method unresolved rather than filling the cell with a guess.

Keep fabric claims separate from what images show

Save the seller's stated fibre composition and any fabric-weight wording with the selected option. Percentages, GSM and total garment weight describe different things. A heavier garment is not automatically a heavier fabric because size, sleeve length, panels and decoration affect total weight. If the listing gives a number without units or scope, preserve the wording and mark the missing information.

An image can support observations about colour, visible texture, seams and printed label text. It cannot establish shrinkage, softness after washing, breathability or long-term print adhesion. Do not convert a smooth-looking image into “premium cotton” or “will not fade.” If those claims matter, look for product-specific evidence and state its limitations. An unsupported performance claim should not decide the comparison merely because it sounds confident.

Inspect the graphic as part of the selected option

Check which sides have artwork and whether the image set represents the chosen design. For a front print, note its position relative to the collar and side seams. For a back print, ask for a full back view before judging a small close-up. A tilted or folded shirt can make alignment look different, so keep camera angle and garment placement in mind when describing a visible issue.

Useful observable checks include missing elements, obvious spelling differences from the selected listing, cracks already visible on arrival, uneven placement and a design that belongs to another option. None of these checks proves future wash resistance. If print size matters, define a measurement between clear endpoints. Do not estimate centimetres from an image unless it contains a suitable scale in the same plane.

Apply the method to the catalogue references

The Acne Studios Longsleeve entry appeared at a historical $16.24 in the site's July snapshot. The Polo Ralph Lauren Long Sleeve entry appeared at $24.81. Those figures used a dated reference conversion and are not current purchase prices or delivered costs. Both examples belong to the broader top category; neither should be presented as a short-sleeve T-shirt merely to fit a keyword.

For either example, establish sleeve type, selected design, actual measurements and what the current destination offers. The Polo entry's destination may be a catalogue search, so identify the exact item before matching its records. If a search produces a different garment, create a separate row. Preserve the historical entry without borrowing its image, price or title for the replacement. Clear identity is more valuable than maintaining a misleadingly complete shortlist.

Record a price that can be reproduced

Select colour, size, design and quantity, then save the displayed amount and currency. Check whether the source shows a range, a quantity-dependent price or a discount with conditions. Keep domestic delivery and optional services in separate fields. Do not write a coupon-adjusted total unless the coupon currently applies to that exact order. A screenshot without the option state is weak price evidence.

PikoBuy's published workflow separates purchasing from later international shipping. Keep that distinction in your article notes and personal worksheet. A shirt's low item price does not tell you its share of a future parcel charge. Before warehouse arrival, weight may be unknown. After packing, use the actual parcel information and the selected route's billing rules rather than multiplying every garment by an assumed freight rate.

Write a useful warehouse request

Ask first for confirmation of garment identity, selected colour, size label and quantity. Then choose any detail that could change the decision: a full front and back view, a label close-up, a print detail or one defined measurement. A request such as “lay the top flat and show the tape straight across the chest between the underarm seams in centimetres” has a clear purpose and method.

Record whether the evidence actually follows the requested method. If a sleeve photo starts at the collar instead of the shoulder seam, label it accordingly. Do not compare it as though the endpoint were unchanged. PikoBuy's official guide describes warehouse photography and visible defect checking; it does not justify assuming a fixed number of photos, unrestricted measurements or independent material testing for every garment.

Make the comparison useful after delivery

Keep separate columns for pre-order seller information, warehouse observations and your own later experience. If you measure or wash the garment after delivery, record the method and conditions before drawing conclusions. A personal observation applies to that item and use; it should not become a guarantee for future batches. Never add a customer testimonial unless a real review and its context can be verified.
